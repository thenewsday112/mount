After Josh Jacobs was arrested, three other women told police he had abused them, too

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

While police were investigating Josh Jacobs following a domestic violence accusation in May, multiple other women reported similar incidents of Jacobs abusing them.

NBC 26 in Green Bay received previously unreleased files in connection with the Jacobs case from the Hobart-Lawrence Police Department late Friday, and those files showed that officers heard from at least three other women who said they had been victims of violence at the hands of Jacobs.

One woman contacted police in June after hearing of Jacobs' May arrest, saying she had been the victim of a similar incident with Jacobs years earlier and was reporting it to police for the first time because she wanted people to believe the woman accusing him in May. That woman said that in January of 2022, Jacobs slapped her across the face, grabbed her by the hair, threw her onto a bed, pinned her down, threw her onto the floor, spit on her and grabbed her by the throat. She also said Jacobs took her phone as she attempted to leave his home.

Another woman also contacted police later in June and said that in April of 2021, Jacobs grabbed her by the arms, pushed her against a wall near a window and threw her belongings outside the home. She said she suffered bruising to her arms and pain and swelling in her rib area. She said she had reported it to the police in a different municipality, and the records released by Hobart-Lawrence Police to NBC 26 indicate that they confirmed with another police department that it did receive that report, but Jacobs apparently was not arrested because the woman had no visible signs of injury.

A third woman told Hobart/Lawrence Police in July that in May of 2022, Jacobs placed his hands on her head and neck, dragged her, scratched her chest, and took her phone. She said she suffered bruising to her arm and scratches to her chest. She said she did not report the incident to police or seek medical attention because Jacobs had told her she could be deported if she had any contact with the authorities.

Jacobs eventually pleaded no contest to misdemeanor battery and criminal damage to property in connection with the May arrest. The NFL placed him on paid leave for the first six games of the season, then allowed him to play starting in Week 7. Jacobs remains subject to a potential NFL suspension for violating the league's personal conduct policy, and the accusations brought by the three other women could be considered by the NFL in determining league discipline.

Night on a Spooky Planet

Explanation: What spooky planet is this? Planet Earth on a dark and stormy night, of course. The ghostly scene was photographed at Hverir, a geothermally active area found along northeastern Iceland’s volcanic landscape. Triggered by solar activity, geomagnetic storms have unleashed energetic particles trapped in Earth’s magnetic field. Penetrating the planet’s upper atmosphere at high latitudes, the particles create auroral displays in the starry night sky. Ghostly towers of steam and gas are venting from local fumaroles to dance against the eerie, greenish northern lights. For now, auroral activity on planet Earth remains in a high state as the Sun is only in early phases of decline from the peak of its solar activity cycle. And soon, other ghostly apparitions may dance in your neighborhood. Happy Halloween!

Wall Street ended October with a brutal selloff, wiping roughly $2 trillion from the Nasdaq-100’s market value as investors questioned whether the artificial intelligence boom could justify its enormous costs.

The damage was concentrated in technology stocks. Intel collapsed nearly 29% on Friday, Reddit fell 29%, and Google parent Alphabet lost more than $500 billion in market value on Thursday alone.

China’s AI bubble burst last week, crashing most of its tech stocks. Is the same pattern now unfolding on Wall Street?

Why Did the US Stock Market Crash?

The immediate trigger was disappointing corporate earnings. Several major technology companies revealed how much money they were spending on AI, while others warned of slowing growth.

Investors were already nervous about elevated stock valuations. Growing inflation and indications that the Fed will increase interest rates in December added pressure.

US Tech Stocks See Massive Crash Over the Last Week of October 2026

Google: AI Spending Is Eating Into Cash

Alphabet shares lost nearly 13% during the week after its quarterly results disappointed investors.

The company reported $9.1 billion in negative free cash flow as AI infrastructure spending accelerated. Its core search advertising business also missed expectations.

Investors are now questioning how quickly Google’s enormous AI investments will generate enough cash to justify their cost.

Amazon: $220 Billion in Spending

Amazon faces a similar problem.

Despite AWS cloud revenue growing 39%, Amazon reported negative free cash flow of $42.4 billion over the preceding 12 months.

The company expects capital expenditure of approximately $220 billion this year, with even more spending anticipated in 2027.

Google and Amazon Have Negative Cash Flow Due to AI Spending

Intel: A Factory Disaster and Falling Demand

Intel’s collapse had more immediate causes.

An explosion at its Israeli manufacturing facility disrupted production, with an estimated $600 million revenue impact over two quarters.

Meanwhile, rising memory prices weakened PC demand. Intel’s fourth-quarter revenue forecast fell substantially below Wall Street expectations, triggering its steepest daily share-price decline in decades.

Reddit and Software Stocks: AI Threatens Existing Businesses

Reddit lost more than 36% over the week after warning that Google’s AI-generated search answers were reducing traffic to its platform.

Enterprise software companies suffered their own selloff. ServiceNow fell approximately 34% for the week following disappointing earnings and a weaker backlog-growth outlook. Cybersecurity company Fortinet lost around 24% after cutting its services-revenue forecast.

Investors increasingly fear AI could undermine parts of the traditional software business.

Is the AI Bubble Starting to Burst?

There are credible warning signs.

Technology companies are spending hundreds of billions on infrastructure before the full financial returns are clear. JPMorgan expects global AI capital expenditure to reach $900 billion in 2026 alone.

The concern is whether AI revenue can grow fast enough to cover these investments, particularly as companies increasingly borrow money to finance expansion.

However, the underlying businesses continue growing. Microsoft reported strong cloud demand, while Google’s cloud revenue more than doubled.

The current evidence points to an AI valuation correction. A sustained collapse in AI investment would provide stronger evidence of a bursting bubble.

AI is all that matters for markets right now.

Will the Stock Market Keep Crashing?

Technical indicators suggest the selloff could continue, although history offers some perspective.

The Nasdaq-100 closed near 28,385, below its approximately 29,800-point 50-day moving average. Its 200-day average, near 27,900, is the next important level to watch.

A sustained break below that longer-term trendline could indicate deeper weakness. Recovering the 50-day average would improve the technical picture.

Historically, corrections are common. Fidelity data shows the S&P 500 has suffered declines of at least 10% in nearly half of calendar years since 1980, often without entering a prolonged bear market.

The market also remains divided. The S&P 500 finished the week down just 0.6%, while smaller companies in the Russell 2000 gained 1.3%.

For now, investors should watch whether AI spending forecasts begin falling, whether corporate earnings continue disappointing, and whether selling spreads beyond technology.

Upcoming earnings from AMD and Nvidia will be particularly important. They could reveal whether the AI infrastructure boom remains strong enough to support current valuations.

The next phase of this selloff will depend heavily on what those companies tell investors about demand.

In Reply to Senators’ Amicus Briefs, Austria, Albertina, and Leopold Museum Double Down on Unconstitutionality of HEAR Act

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Republic of Austria, the Albertina Museum, and the Leopold Museum Foundation—the latter two both in Vienna—filed a response Friday to amicus briefs submitted by two US senators, in which they repeat their claim that the updated Holocaust Expropriated Art Recovery (HEAR) Act violates Article III of the US Constitution and the Tenth Amendment.

Austria, the Albertina, and the Leopold are defendants in a suit brought by Timothy Reif, a descendant of Fritz Grünbaum, a Jewish cabaret performer and collector who was killed in a death camp in 1941. Reif is seeking the return of several works by Egon Schiele that he alleges were looted by the Nazis when they seized Grünbaum’s collection. The defendants dispute the claim, arguing that the works in question are part of the same cache that was exported from Austria by Grünbaum’s sister-in-law in the 1930s and then sold to a Swiss dealer in the 1950s.

Although the defendants had previously argued in several motions that the HEAR Act is unconstitutional, this new filing responds to briefs submitted last month by the Department of Justice and by Senators Richard Blumenthal (Democrat of Connecticut) and John Cornyn (Republican of Texas). Those briefs presented additional legal precedent supporting the constitutionality of the HEAR Act. Friday’s response addresses each of those cases.

Though the defendants “respect the work of the Senators and the Government, and their attention to trying to remedy at least some of the horrors of the Holocaust,” they believe that in passing the amended version of HEAR, “Congress exceeded its constitutional bounds,” the filing states.

Blumenthal and Cornyn did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

At the heart of the response are the 2025 amendments to the HEAR Act, which President Donald Trump signed into law last year. Originally signed in 2016, the act was set to expire in 2026; the amendments extend it indefinitely. Other key changes were meant to address several high-profile cases that had failed in court on technical grounds, including statutes of limitations that vary depending on the state in which a case is brought.

The defendants argue that the HEAR Act stops short of creating a federal law for art restitution, akin to federal copyright law, and in doing so “delegates the substantive means to restitute Nazi-looted art to the states,” the filing reads. Courts are then to rely on applicable state laws, but by eliminating defenses based on technicalities, the 2025 changes dictate how courts should rule on the cases before them.

As a result, the defendants argue, the HEAR Act violates both the Tenth Amendment, which states that any power not explicitly granted to the federal government belongs to the states, and Article III of the Constitution, specifically the separation-of-powers doctrine, which gives courts the authority to decide the cases before them and how to rule on them.

“By forbidding the federal courts from any longer considering these equitable defenses, Congress is intruding on the constitutional right and obligation of Article III courts to ‘decide’ such cases and controversies for themselves,” the defendants’ response reads.

One defense eliminated by the 2025 amendments is laches, under which an otherwise valid claim can be dismissed if the claimant unreasonably delayed bringing it. The change bars courts from conducting “any factfinding as to questions of whether ‘inexcusable delay’ by a plaintiff or the plaintiff’s predecessors caused the loss of critical evidence, resulting in ‘unfair prejudice’ to the current owner of allegedly stolen art,” the response states.

The filing adds, “By outlawing the laches defense in particular, which prevents unprovable allegations of Nazi theft from becoming de facto conclusive presumptions of Nazi theft, the HEAR Act unquestionably does compel particular factfinding in favor of plaintiffs.”

In a phone interview, William L. Charron, an attorney for the defendants, said the purpose of Friday’s filing was to respond directly to the additional arguments for the HEAR Act’s constitutionality raised by the Justice Department and Senators Blumenthal and Cornyn.

“The government and the Senators intervened and involved themselves in this case because they wanted to be heard as to their reasons why they believe the HEAR Act is constitutional, and so this was an opportunity for my clients to respond and explain why they respectfully disagree with those positions and respectfully believe the HEAR Act is unconstitutional,” he told ARTnews.

Charron said he believes Reif v. Republic of Austria can be decided without addressing the constitutionality of the HEAR Act. But if the courts decide they must rule on that question, he said, it is the kind of case that could eventually make its way to the Supreme Court.

“I believe these are important and serious issues,” Charron said of the arguments raised in the filing. “We have given the Court a number of arguments and grounds to dismiss the case without having to rule upon these constitutional questions. But if the Court decides that it does need to address the constitutionality of the HEAR Act, I certainly hope that it seriously considers the arguments that have been raised in this filing.”

WOVEN BAMBOO pairs with CROCHETED TEXTILES IN CHAIR SCULPTURE

 

Designed by Peiyang (Camille) Li, She Blooms in the Dark is a glowing seating sculpture that examines emotional vulnerability and protective spatial thresholds. The project pairs hand-crocheted textile craft with traditional bent-wood furniture techniques, mounting an array of individual blossoms across the open-lattice framework of an oversized, woven bamboo armchair.


SHE BLOOMS IN THE DARK: Day Time View | all images ©Kexin Liu

 

 

UV-REACTIVE YARN BLOOMS STORE DAYLIGHT TO EMIT AMBIENT GLOW

 

The sculptural seat by designer Peiyang (Camille) Li functions simultaneously as a functional chair and a semi-enclosed personal retreat, utilizing a curved, basket-like silhouette to delineate an intimate interior microclimate for solitary reflection. Each floral element is crocheted by hand using phosphorescent, UV-reactive yarn engineered to absorb daylight and emit a soft, low-intensity glow in dark environments. By forgoing high-output electric fixtures in favor of photoluminescent fibers, the textile piece activates after dark, transforming the structural bamboo envelope into an illuminated shelter where soft woven media counterbalance the rigid natural framework.

Kelly Clarkson Wins One & Loses One at 2026 Daytime Emmys

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Kelly Clarkson Show won outstanding daytime talk series at the 2026 Daytime Emmys on Friday (Oct. 30), though its star, Kelly Clarkson, lost outstanding daytime talk series host to Sherri Shepherd for Sherri.

Clarkson won in one or both of those categories in six of the show’s seven seasons on the air. She has won a total of nine Daytime Emmys in seven years – five in the talk series category and four for talk series host – compared to three Grammys in a recording career which has lasted more than 20 years. Clarkson wasn’t present at the ceremony, which was held at the Hollywood Palladium for the first time.

Clarkson recently made headlines for an incident at a curiosity shop in Brooklyn where she maintained that she was treated poorly. But “Birdgate” had no bearing on the outcome here. There is just one round of Daytime Emmy voting, which determines both the nominations and the eventual winner. That round closed on June 10, four months before the incident.

The Kelly Clarkson Show won four other awards this year – outstanding directing for a daytime studio non-fiction series; outstanding sound mixing and sound editing for a live daytime program; outstanding art direction/set decoration/scenic design for a daytime program; and outstanding hairstyling and makeup (in a tie with Beyond the Gates).

Those five awards made The Kelly Clarkson Show the most-awarded show of the night. It was followed by General Hospital and Beyond the Gates, with four wins each; The Young and the Restless and A Different Breed, with three wins each; and Be My Guest With Ina Garten, Long Way Home and Macy’s Thanksgiving Parade, with two wins each.

Although The Kelly Clarkson Show ended its seven-season run on Aug. 31, it will be eligible again for the Daytime Emmys that are presented in 2027. The National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences adheres to calendar-year eligibility, so the eight months the show aired in 2026 will be reflected in the Daytime Emmy nominations that are announced in 2027. (Whether a show that is off-the-air can draw enough voter support to be nominated is another question.)

Ray Chew won outstanding music direction and composition for a daytime program for Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade. His award was accepted by his wife, who explained that he was busy working on Dancing With the Stars. Chew has also served in music direction capacities on American Idol, Saturday Night Live, the Emmy Awards, the BET Awards and the Grammy Awards’ Premiere Ceremony.

Keke Palmer won her first Daytime Emmy – outstanding daytime short form program as producer of Glam Through the Ages. Palmer has won two Primetime Emmys, for Keke Palmer’s Turnt Up With the Taylors and the reboot of Password.

Tracee Ellis Ross won outstanding travel and adventure program for Solo Traveling With Tracee Ellis Ross. It’s her first Emmy Award (Daytime or Primetime) following six Primetime Emmy nods for ABC’s blackish. Ross’ legendary mom, Diana Ross, has yet to win an Emmy.

Off Script With The Hollywood Reporter won outstanding arts and popular culture program, beating a pair of programs produced by their sister brand, Variety – Variety & CNN Actors on Actors and Variety Studio: Actors on Actors.

Star Jones won her first Daytime Emmy in a 35-year TV career – outstanding daytime personality – daily for Divorce Court, which she won alongside Corey Jovan. Jones thanked two late legends who she described as “her two mentors – Johnnie Cochran, who taught me about justice, and Barbara Walters, who taught me to ask the tough question.”

General Hospital won outstanding daytime drama series for the sixth consecutive year – and 19th time overall, both records. Runner-up for most total wins in the category is The Young and The Restless, with 11 wins.

Genie Francis received a lifetime achievement award to celebrate her soon-to-be 50th anniversary playing Laura Spencer on General Hospital. Her co-star on that show, Anthony Geary, the other half of the Luke & Laura storyline which took daytime drama to new heights in the early 1980s, died in December 2025. He was remembered in the In Memoriam segment alongside critic Gene Shalit and such other actors as June Lockhart and Sally Kirkland.

This is the second year in a row that the Daytime Emmys didn’t air on linear TV. Instead, the ceremony streamed live on the NATAS YouTube channel at YouTube.com/@theemmys and watch.TheEmmys.tv.
