Against Cultural Flattening: Creative Retreats as the New Way to Travel

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This past March, The Wall Street Journal‘s “Craft Retreats Are the New Burnout Cure” caught our attention. It’s something we’ve noticed among friends, too: traveling to Sicily to finally take up basketry from a favorite weaver, making cheese and yogurt in the Basque mountains, and so forth. This style of travel is not for the passive vacationer. Not for the beachcomber, and not necessarily for the whole family. These curated retreats are for the fidgety, for those drawn to hands-on learning—the artist residency for the non- or nascent artist.

“I’ve known what it feels like to lack inspiration and feel stagnant,” writes Alice Katter, founder of Out of Office Network, which hosts creative retreats at an agriturismo in Italy. “It’s a response to a cultural flattening,” she continues. “As everything becomes algorithmic, online, optimized, and the same…”

The following immersive, hands-on travel experiences are among the most appealing we’ve uncovered yet.

Above: Anna Tasca Lanza in Sicily, Italy,

Years ago, Anna Tasca Lanza in Sicily hosted a weaving workshop with Studio AMOS, and the school has been on our radar ever since. While its retreats typically center on food—past hosts have included David Tanis, Julia Sherman, and Rachel Roddy—it occasionally ventures into craft, as with the weaving workshop and an upcoming sketching and Drawing retreat with Samantha Dion Baker. Photograph from Anna Tasca Lanza by Chiara Leto and Erika Pino for The Preserve Journal.

Above: Quilting at an Ace Camp Travel retreat.

Ace Camp Travel hosts creative retreats around the world: block printing in Jaipur, India; painting in Andalusia, Spain; basket weaving in the Stockholm Archipelago, Sweden; embroidery in Chiang Mai, Thailand; and many more. The premise is simple: travel centered on hands-on learning, lectures, and demonstrations led by local experts and practitioners.

Above: Out of Office Network creative retreat at Le Cergue, Italy.

Out of Office Network began as a digital platform supporting creative expression, founded by Alice Katter, who later developed a creative residency program at Le Cerque, an agriturismo outside of Rome. Unlike artist residencies, where established artists focus on and present a single project, Out of Office’s offerings might look more like a three-day botanical dye intensive with a textile designer. Katter also coordinates artist-led workshops in Barcelona, including silversmithing with Anna Ruiz Planella, indigo dyeing with Carlota Simó, and furniture restoration with Audrey Pogu.

Above: DescoverArtists at the natural dye retreat.

DescoverArtists is a global community of creatives that offers intimate, shared-home retreats for practicing artists, integrating workshops focused on skill development and exchange. They also host a Natural Dye Retreat on Chios, Greece, with George Petsikopoulos, created for just six selected participants (application required) and equally open to beginners, artists, and designers alike.

Above: Inside the Praktyka Creative Retreat in North Devon.

Praktyka is a creative retreat on the North Devon coast founded by Ania Wawrzkowicz and Henry Trew in collaboration with Niall Maxwell of Rural Office Architects (see our post on A Low-Impact Family Home in Surrey and Norfolk Remodel with Tithe and Piggery). Guests stay in architectural cabins while taking part in art workshops. Unlike many retreats, Praktyka welcomes group stays that include children. It also hosts curated programs that bring together participants from around the world, such as Coastal Walking and Printmaking, and a Mother’s Retreat featuring clay and jewelry making.

Above: Madame Voyage French Retreats in southern France.

Madame Voyage creates what it calls “slow travel French retreats.” Founded by two women from France, the company hosts week-long retreats in Provence, Toulouse, and along the Riviera. The retreats span both culinary and creative, with workshops in ceramics, floral design, watercolor, and more.

Coinbase Says Customer Funds Safe After Analytics Vendor Contact-Data Incident

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Coinbase said an incident involving analytics provider Amplitude affected contact information associated with a small subset of Coinbase customers. For exchange users, the practical response is to verify contact through bookmarked official channels rather than links in unexpected messages.

Coinbase said an incident involving analytics provider Amplitude affected contact information associated with a small subset of Coinbase customers. The exchange stated that its own systems had not been accessed without authorization and that customer funds remained safe. It also said affected users would receive relevant notification. Those assertions distinguish the disclosed third-party exposure from an attack that directly drained wallets or compromised exchange custody, although the company did not provide a comprehensive public breakdown of affected data fields or customer counts.

The event and the market response

Contact details can still create real security problems. Fraudsters may use an exposed email address or phone number to write convincing impersonation messages, attempt account recovery or pose as support staff. The harm often arrives after the original incident, when an attacker combines limited information with urgent payment instructions or a request to disclose authentication codes. A customer does not need to lose a private key in the initial incident to face heightened phishing risk later. Users should therefore treat unexpected security messages with care even if their account balances look normal.

Why the development matters to the crypto market

For exchange users, the practical response is to verify contact through bookmarked official channels rather than links in unexpected messages. Turning on strong authentication, reviewing recent sign-ins and being cautious about requests for recovery phrases can reduce exposure to social engineering. These are sensible precautions even when an exchange says it has not suffered a direct intrusion. The broader market takeaway is that custody security and personal-data security are related but distinct; a platform can protect assets while a vendor still leaks information.

The strongest security response usually looks operational rather than spectacular: inventory exposed systems, restrict unnecessary access, apply verified patches, monitor unusual activity and explain the incident accurately to affected users. Claims of an exploit should not be made when only a possible vulnerability has been identified. At the same time, the absence of a confirmed theft does not eliminate the need for remediation. Trust comes from a provider’s ability to document its response and maintain reliable controls across the third parties and components on which its service depends.

Regulated institutions assessing digital assets must plan for both malicious transactions and ordinary operational failure. A secure key may be unusable after a hardware problem; a correctly signed payment can still reach the wrong destination; a trusted software component may later be compromised. Defense therefore combines cryptography with governance, recovery, vendor oversight and clear human approval. A new security tool or partnership can improve one part of that system without becoming a universal solution. Asking exactly which threats are addressed is more useful than declaring the technology safe or unsafe.

What the headline does not establish

The disclosure highlights supply-chain dependence. Exchanges use outside vendors for analytics, communication, support and other services, and some of those partners may process information that is valuable to attackers. Data minimization, retention controls, vendor audits and prompt notification are therefore part of exchange security alongside cold storage and transaction signing. Coinbase’s claim that customer funds are unaffected is important, but the available information leaves limits around exact scope. It would be inappropriate to repeat unverified rumors about a precise number of impacted customers as fact.

What to watch next

Watch for a more detailed incident statement about affected fields, notification completion and remedial vendor controls. Until then, readers should avoid unverified counts and use official Coinbase support channels to assess any account-specific risk.

The OCC's final GENIUS Act rule sets a 5% cash floor and a 40% cap on any one counterparty. The Fed's open proposal uses neither figure, and its comment window runs until November 30.

Issuers now face two sets of reserve rules that share a redemption deadline and differ on almost everything else.

Ask a stablecoin issuer what its reserves must look like in 2027, and the honest answer is now: it depends which regulator you ask.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) gave its answer on October 21, the final rule implementing the GENIUS Act. Our October 19 piece on staggered agency filings expected that rule "before the end of October," and it came two days later. The rule sets specific numbers: at least 5% of reserves in demand deposits or similar instant-access funds, and no more than 40% of reserves with a single counterparty.

The Federal Reserve's answer is still a proposal. Its GENIUS Act rulemaking remains open for comment until November 30, and it contains neither of those numbers. Until the Fed finalizes its rule, any company planning to issue under both agencies' rules, or deciding which one to apply to, is working with one fixed set of numbers and one open-ended set of principles.

What The OCC Locked In On October 21

The OCC's final rule was published in the Federal Register on October 23 and takes effect on January 18, 2027, the same day as the GENIUS Act itself. Comptroller Jonathan V. Gould called it "a clear, practical framework for payment stablecoin innovation, with strong standards for reserve backing, redemption, and risk management."

The reserve rules are looser than the March proposal in several places:

  • Cash floor: at least 5% of reserve assets must sit in demand deposits, insured shares or Federal Reserve Bank balances. The proposal asked for 10%.
  • Counterparty cap: no more than 40% of reserves with one counterparty. Exposures to the U.S. government, qualifying custodians and centrally cleared repo are exempt.
  • Dropped requirements: the proposed weekly liquidity limits and a 20-day weighted-average-maturity requirement were removed.
  • Excess reserves: issuers can now withdraw them at any time after due diligence, instead of on a monthly schedule.

The OCC also removed the rebuttable presumption that some affiliate yield arrangements break the GENIUS Act's ban on paying interest, though it kept its broader anti-evasion powers. The agency received about 328 comments.

For existing issuers, the timeline is short. Under OCC Bulletin 2026-51, firms already operating in the United States that file a "substantially complete" application by January 18 can generally expect a waiver of the prior-approval requirement.

Where The Fed's Open Proposal Stands

The Fed released its two proposals on September 24. They were published in the Federal Register on September 29 under Docket No. R-1899, and comments are due November 30.

The main requirements proposal would require Board-supervised issuers to "fully back their stablecoins with certain permissible reserve assets". The reserves' fair value would have to equal or exceed the stablecoins' outstanding value at all times. Permissible assets include:

  • coins and currency
  • Federal Reserve Bank balances
  • eligible deposits
  • short-dated Treasuries and related repurchase agreements
  • funds that hold only those assets

On diversification, however, the Fed sets no numbers. Reserves must be "sufficiently diverse" to keep one-to-one backing at all times, and issuers must manage concentration in uninsured deposits and repo counterparties. The Board says it generally expects reserves to be mostly Treasuries, but it sets no cash floor and no counterparty percentage.

Same Two-Day Clock, Different Escape Hatches

Redemption is where the two agencies agree most closely. Both define timely redemption as no later than two business days after a request. The difference lies in what happens under stress.

The OCC's final rule:

  • An issuer must notify the OCC within 24 hours if redemption requests exceed 10% of outstanding issuance in a single 24-hour period.
  • The issuer may then ask for an extension, which the OCC can grant or refuse.
  • The proposed automatic extension to seven calendar days is gone, after commenters warned it could deepen a run.

The Fed's proposal:

  • It has no 10% trigger.
  • The Board may extend the deadline at its discretion for safety-and-soundness, financial-stability or public-interest reasons.
  • Separate safe harbours cover delays caused by anti-money-laundering and sanctions reviews, or by events outside the issuer's control.

The FDIC, in its own proposal, uses a third approach: issuers would report on their own when redemption requests exceed 10% of issuance within 24 hours.

The Reserve Floors Side By Side

The clearest conflict is not about how much cash an issuer must hold, but how each agency treats bank deposits.

Under the OCC rule, at least 5% of reserves must be held in demand deposits or similar balances. That is a direct requirement to hold bank money.

The Fed proposal has no deposit floor. Instead, it would charge 2% capital against reserves held as uninsured deposits at an insured bank, and the same charge applies to undercollateralized reverse repo. One agency requires a minimum amount in deposits, and the other makes issuers hold extra capital against the deposits they choose to keep.

Concentration works the same way. An issuer with 60% of its deposit reserves at one bank would breach the OCC's 40% cap, unless one of the custodian or government exemptions applies. Under the Fed's current text, that issuer would face only a judgment by its supervisor about whether it has managed concentration risk properly.

The rules for a reserve shortfall also differ:

  • OCC: the issuer must stop issuing immediately, and must start liquidating reserves only if the gap lasts 15 consecutive business days.
  • Fed (proposed): the issuer must notify the Board within 24 hours and file a remediation plan, and liquidation must start by the end of the business day after the plan is filed unless backing has been restored.

Capital Is Where The Gap Widens

The difference is largest on capital. The OCC sets an individualized capital requirement measured in GAAP tangible equity. It has a $5 million floor that now lasts for the issuer's whole life, plus an operational backstop equal to 12 months of total expenses, up to half of which can be funded with qualifying subordinated debt.

The Fed proposes a fixed formula. On top of the 2% charges on deposits and repo, it adds an operational-risk charge on outstanding issuance:

  • 2% on the first $20 billion
  • 1.5% on the next $30 billion
  • 1% on anything above $50 billion

It also adds 25% of three-year average revenue from sources other than reserves, adjusted by a loss scalar.

For an issuer with $50 billion in circulation, the issuance charge alone works out to $850 million under the Fed's proposal, before the revenue add-on. The OCC rule has no comparable fixed figure. Its capital depends on the issuer's own risk profile, with the $5 million floor as the only set minimum.

What Issuers Can Do Before November 30

For now, the OCC's figures are final and the Fed's are not. Issuers and banks that hold reserves can still argue for changes until the Fed's comment period closes on November 30, and the Fed has asked 277 questions.

The decisions that matter most are clear from the comparison above:

  • Deposits: whether the Fed adds a cash floor like the OCC's or keeps charging capital against uninsured deposits.
  • Concentration: whether its principles-based approach gains a numerical cap.
  • Capital: whether its standardized formula survives against the OCC's individualized approach.

Both sets of rules apply from the same date, January 18, 2027. If the Fed's final rule keeps its current approach, the United States will begin regulating stablecoins with a common redemption deadline and two different reserve rulebooks, decided by which agency charters the issuer.

Rush Street Interactive kept its 2026 revenue guidance but said October trading points to the low end of the range, after raising its outlook a quarter earlier. With online casino at 75% of revenue, the warning is fresh operator evidence that US online casino growth is cooling heading into the fourth quarter.

The third-quarter results of BetRivers owner Rush Street Interactive came out on October 28. The guidance range it reaffirmed implies 38% to 41% growth for 2026. Adjusted EBITDA guidance stays at $245 million to $265 million, with October tracking toward the midpoint of that range.

The warning breaks a pattern from the summer. In the second quarter, reported July 29, the company posted record revenue of $393.8 million, up 46%, and raised its full-year guidance. Third-quarter sales were up from $277.9 million a year earlier but below the second-quarter total. Growth also slowed from the 46% pace of the prior quarter.

Profits outpaced revenue as casino share kept rising

Net income rose 93% to $28.7 million, from $14.8 million. Adjusted EBITDA rose 73% to $62.2 million, from $36.0 million. Sales and marketing expense grew 43% to $56.0 million, from $39.0 million a year earlier.

The online casino share of revenue rose from 72% in the second quarter. That weighting makes the company a close read on US casino demand. The 32% growth in casino revenue also includes Latin America, so it is not a pure US figure.

Player numbers kept growing. North American monthly active users reached a record 300,572, up 34% year over year. Latin American users rose 46% to 607,308, for a total of 907,881.

Management blames a slower market, not lost share

Management blamed slower growth in the North American online casino market, not lost market share, for revenue tracking toward the low end of guidance. Michigan, in their view, has also become more competitive. Statewide figures from Michigan are consistent with a softer market. In September, Michigan iGaming adjusted gross receipts came to $288.15 million, 0.9% lower than in August.

The shares fell 1.82% in regular trading on October 28 to $15.13. After hours, the stock moved below its 52-week low. The company bought back about $14 million of stock during the quarter and another $11 million after it ended. That leaves $75 million under its repurchase authorization.

Juventus will stay together in a training retreat at the JTC Continassa from Friday 30 October until Sunday's game against Napoli, after a blunt meeting between Giovanni Carnevali, the players and Luciano Spalletti. That home fixture is now widely seen as decisive for the coach's future and the direction of the season.

A direct meeting with chief executive Giovanni Carnevali has put the Juventus players and their coach under heavy pressure before Napoli’s visit.

The trigger was the loss in Genoa. Kingsley Ehizibue struck in the 12th minute, and Tommaso Baldanzi added a second in the 28th. Spalletti apologised to the supporters afterwards. He said his side had been beaten in every area. He added that they had to raise their physical level and fighting spirit.

Carnevali backs the staff but demands results

The squad travelled back by train overnight. The players went to the Continassa the next morning. Management spoke to them there before training. Carnevali reminded them of their responsibilities to the owners, the club and the fans. He said the coaching staff still had the club’s trust, Adnkronos reported. He also asked for a strong and concrete reaction on the pitch. With the club’s agreement, Spalletti then chose to keep the players together at the training centre until kick-off.

Clubs rarely call this kind of retreat. It shows how serious the mood is at the Continassa. The message from the top is twofold. Public backing for the coaching staff comes paired with pressure for results. That places heavy weight on a single fixture.

Identical records hide a gap in front of goal

Both sides have four wins, two draws and three defeats from nine games, Corriere dello Sport reported. Napoli sit seventh and Juventus eighth.

The gap lies in front of goal. Napoli have scored 17 times. Juventus have managed 11. Juventus have conceded only 10, however. Those figures are consistent with a side whose problems lie more in attack than in defence. A reaction against Napoli would need goals as well as fighting spirit.

Allegri later said he had decided to drop it. That leaves Juventus facing a Napoli side without Allegri in charge at a moment when their own coach is under pressure.

The last meeting between the sides in Turin, on 25 January 2026, ended in a 3-0 Juventus win at the Allianz Stadium.

The Round 4 tie began at 20:05 on Thursday 29 October and needed only two games. KC took the first game in 28 minutes and closed out the second in 37.

The result ends a run of two straight defeats. An opening victory over Anyone’s Legend had been followed by single-game losses in Rounds 2 and 3. They now have three games won and two lost in the event.

CFO were the higher seed, so they had first choice for game one. They took first pick, which put KC on the blue side. The opener was one-sided, with KC finishing on 18 kills to CFO’s three, according to GosuGamers.

CFO’s exit ends the LCP’s run at Worlds 2026

Riot Games uses a five-round Swiss format. A team that reaches three match losses is knocked out. For CFO, this was a third defeat. Their opening win over Team Liquid was followed by losses to Movistar KOI and T1.

Before Worlds, CFO’s last two results came in LCP Split 3 2026: a 3-1 win over MVK Esports and a 0-3 defeat to Team Secret Whales.

The Elo power rankings show a wide gap between the sides. KC are 10th on a rating of 1796. CFO are 54th on 1638. The teams had met before, at the First Stand 2025 knockout stage on 15 March 2025, when KC won 3-2.

Karmine Corp face a winner-takes-all best-of-three

KC reached the Swiss Stage through the Play-In. They opened it on 15 October 2026 by edging Cloud9 in a five-game series. They then beat MVK Esports 3-0 in both the Upper Bracket Final and the Grand Final. Their roster is Canna, Yike, Busio, Caliste and Kyeahoo.

A team goes through to the Knockout Stage once it reaches three match wins. One more victory would send KC through, while one more defeat would end their campaign. The Knockout Stage is split between Allen and Brooklyn, New York. Quarter-finals run from 3 to 6 November and start at 10.00pm UTC.
