Daily pool data shows Sprout holders moved more than 300 ZEC out in a September rush, then almost stopped. The last withdrawal was on October 19.

Unless someone acts in the next few days, nearly all of what remains will be unspendable at block 3,543,405.

Most coverage of Zcash's upcoming Sprout freeze quotes the same figure, about 22,000 ZEC. That number is accurate, but it is a single snapshot. It doesn't show whether the people who own those coins are doing anything about the deadline.

We checked the pool every day, and the answer so far is no.

According to ZecStats' daily value-pool records, Sprout has held exactly 22,263.34162335 ZEC since October 19. That is 11 straight days, and more than 12,000 blocks, without a single zatoshi leaving the pool. Our October 25 piece recorded the drop from 22,309 ZEC to 22,263 ZEC. Since then the balance hasn't moved.

What The Daily Numbers Show

ZecStats publishes a daily CSV of every Zcash value pool, using the last block of each UTC day. Its figures follow the valuePools field that a full node returns through the getblockchaininfo call. The archive starts on July 30, 2026, and gives a 92-day picture of the Sprout pool.

Across those 92 days, about 374 ZEC, or 1.65% of the pool, left Sprout. That is an average of roughly 4 ZEC a day. The average hides the real pattern, though: the pool moved on only 15 of the 92 days.

A September Rush, Then Almost Nothing

Most of the withdrawals came in a few large transfers in September:

  • September 16: 99.998 ZEC left the pool.
  • September 23: 49.09 ZEC left.
  • September 25: 112.41 ZEC left, the largest single day in the record.

Together with smaller moves on August 31, September 17 and September 19, the 30 days to September 29 saw 310.5 ZEC leave Sprout. That is about 10 ZEC a day.

October was much quieter. Apart from one withdrawal of 46.05 ZEC on October 19, the month's activity was small change:

  • 0.0036 ZEC on October 2
  • 1.33 ZEC on October 6
  • 0.05 ZEC on October 18

Since October 19, there has been no movement at all.

At September's pace, about 70 ZEC more would leave before activation. At October's pace, it would be well under 15 ZEC. On the last 11 days' pace, the answer is zero.

What Changes At Block 3,543,405

The deadline is set in ZIP 259, which defines how the NU7 network upgrade is deployed. It sets the mainnet activation height at 3,543,405.

NU7 does not introduce a new transaction format. Instead, after activation, version 4 transactions become invalid, while versions 5 and 6 remain valid. Sprout's design is the problem. As ZIP 259 puts it: "Because Sprout transfers can only be represented by version 4 transactions, funds remaining in the Sprout pool become unspendable."

The coins would not be destroyed, and NU7 does not change Zcash's supply cap. They would simply be stuck in a pool that no valid transaction can spend from. Today the stranded amount would be 22,263 ZEC, about 0.13% of the roughly 17.01 million ZEC in circulation.

The rest of the upgrade is larger:

  • block target spacing drops from 75 seconds to 25 seconds
  • new per-pool and global limits on shielded actions
  • the Network Sustainability Mechanism
  • removal of 60% of transaction fees from circulation

ZIP 259 also skips Zcash's usual three-month gap between the first supporting software release and activation. Operators running unsupported software could stay online and follow a pre-NU7 chain.

Why The Countdown Lands Late On November 5

As of ZecStats' latest update on October 30 at 13:09 UTC, the chain was at block 3,535,877. That leaves 7,528 blocks until activation.

In October, the network averaged about 1,147 blocks a day, or one roughly every 75.3 seconds. At that rate, block 3,543,405 arrives around 02:00–03:00 UTC on November 6. That is the evening of November 5 in the Americas and the early hours of November 6 in Europe and Africa.

Small changes in block times could move that window by a few hours. Holders who plan to move funds on the final day risk missing the cutoff.

Who Still Has Time To Move

In practice, getting funds out of Sprout means sending a version 4 transaction that moves them into a newer pool before activation. Users need software that can still build Sprout transactions and a synced node or wallet that recognizes their old notes.

The September withdrawals show that some holders are still paying attention. Several were round amounts, such as 99.998 ZEC, which is consistent with someone moving 100 ZEC and paying a fee. The 11-day pause since October 19 suggests something else: most of the remaining 22,263 ZEC may belong to keys that are lost, forgotten or no longer used.

That has been part of the debate about Sprout for years. A pool that has been shrinking slowly since Zcash's early days is now on a fixed timetable. The daily data indicates that most of its remaining balance is not being moved.

Why A Static Figure Misses The Story

Every report that cites "about 22,000 ZEC at risk" is technically correct. The more useful figure for anyone tracking the upgrade is the rate of change, and right now that rate is zero.

The useful thing to watch over the next week is whether the Sprout line in ZecStats' daily data moves again before block 3,543,405. If it doesn't, Zcash will activate NU7 with about 22,263 ZEC permanently locked in its first shielded pool. That would be one of the largest deliberate freezes of user funds by any major blockchain upgrade, and nearly all of it would be coins whose owners did not move them in time.

The Time Change Wrecks My Entire Family Every Single Year

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Ines Cobos Jurado/Moment/Getty Images

Every year, someone cheerfully announces that daylight saving time is ending as if we have all just won an extra hour of sleep. And every year, I wonder who exactly is receiving this free hour, because it is certainly not the parents.

In my house, the time change does not feel like gaining an hour. It feels like someone picked up our entire routine, shook it like an Etch A Sketch, and handed it back with a smile.

Dinner is suddenly happening at the wrong time. Bedtime feels suspicious. Morning arrives before anyone’s brain has agreed to participate. By 5:30 p.m., it’s pitch-black outside and my body is convinced we have missed bedtime by at least three business days.

People tend to talk about the time change as a problem for parents of babies and toddlers. There are entire survival guides devoted to shifting naps by 15-minute increments so a two-year-old does not greet the day at 4:12 a.m.

But once your child gets older, everyone seems to assume the problem disappears. It does not disappear. The child just gets taller and develops stronger opinions about why bedtime is a government conspiracy.

My son is 11 now. He is not a toddler. He can read a clock. He understands, intellectually, that we moved it back one hour. None of that means his body, brain, appetite, attention span, or emotional regulation received the memo.

The clock may say 7:00, but his nervous system is operating in its own private time zone… and frankly, so is mine.

That is the part I think we miss when we talk about older kids and daylight saving time. We treat it like a scheduling inconvenience instead of what it really is for some families: a disruption to the external cues that help our brains know what comes next.

My son may be old enough to understand what happened, but understanding is not the same as adjusting. Older kids still have circadian rhythms. They still rely on environmental cues. ADHD does not pack its bags and leave just because a child can make his own sandwich. Anxiety does not check the calendar and decide middle schoolers are too mature to be affected by sleep disruption.

And, for the record, adults are not magically immune either. I am 42 years old, and the time change absolutely wrecks me. It takes one dark afternoon for my motivation to crawl into a cave. Suddenly, I am tired at 6:00 p.m., wide awake when I should be sleeping, and personally offended by the sun for abandoning us before dinner.

My brain already has a complicated relationship with time. Moving every clock in the house and changing when daylight appears is not the tiny administrative update society pretends it is.

Meanwhile, life does not pause for adjustment. School still starts at the same number on the clock. Homework still needs to happen. Dinner still needs to materialize through the ancient magic of a mother staring into the refrigerator and hoping the ingredients will organize themselves.

Everyone is more tired, less patient, and somehow surprised that the evening is not going smoothly.

This is usually when parents start blaming themselves.

We think the routine is failing because we have become inconsistent. We wonder why our older child is suddenly moodier, more impulsive, harder to wake, or completely done with humanity by late afternoon. We tell ourselves they should be able to handle a one-hour change.

But one hour is not always just one hour. It is darkness arriving before our brains expect it. It is hunger, attention, sleep, and transitions all showing up slightly out of order. It is the fragile after-school window getting even narrower. It is a child holding it together through seven hours of school and arriving home with absolutely nothing left in the emotional gas tank. It is a neurodivergent parent trying to regulate a neurodivergent child while her own nervous system is also filing a formal complaint.

So this year, I am lowering the bar before the clocks even change. For a few days, dinner can be embarrassingly easy. Bedtime may need to start earlier, even if my middle schooler insists he is basically an adult. Mornings may require more alarms, more light, and fewer unnecessary conversations.

If homework feels harder, emotions run hotter, or we are both staring blankly at each other by 7:00 p.m., I am going to remember that our bodies are adjusting instead of deciding we have suddenly lost every coping skill we ever learned.

I am also refusing to romanticize the whole thing.

Yes, fall is beautiful. Yes, the leaves are gorgeous. Yes, cozy evenings sound lovely in theory. But there is nothing cozy about being trapped in a dark house at 5:45 p.m. with a dysregulated child, an untouched homework assignment, and the dawning realization that bedtime is still hours away.

So if your entire household becomes a little feral when the clocks change, you are not imagining it… and you are not failing.

Keep the routines you can. Add more light, more rest, and more grace where you can find them. Feed everyone something requiring the fewest possible dishes. Give the family a little time to recalibrate.

The clock only moved an hour. Unfortunately, no one told our nervous systems it was supposed to be no big deal.

Danielle Kelly is the host of the ADHD parenting podcast Chaos & Caffeine, which reaches thousands of families each month and was recently named one of the Best Kids With ADHD Podcasts of 2026 by PodRanker.

The Newcastle United exit from the Carabao Cup leaves Matthias Jaissle facing a fair question: are we watching the uneven beginnings of something promising, or a team struggling to establish the foundations it needs?

For now, there is evidence for both views.

Everton should sharpen the scrutinybut it should not settle the verdict.

Jaissle was appointed on 5 August. He has had less than three months in charge, arriving shortly before the competitive season and succeeding a manager whose achievements gave supporters both wonderful memories and legitimate expectations. That is a demanding inheritance. It is also too short a period to judge a managerial appointment conclusively.

The league record offers some perspective. Newcastle have taken 11 points from eight matches, with three wins, two draws and three defeats. They have scored 12 goals and conceded 14. That is an uneven start, rather than evidence of either a breakthrough or a collapse.

There are genuine glimmers of hope. Winning 2–0 at Tottenham demonstrated that this side can deliver away from home. More recently, the victory by the same score at Crystal Palace provided an encouraging response to successive league defeats. Jaissle made five changes for Palace, and several of those introduced contributed directly to the result. That suggests a willingness to reconsider his selections and an ability to produce a response.

Availability also matters.

For the Hull game, Newcastle had nine players missing, while Joelinton, Anthony Elanga and Jacob Ramsey have been among those working their way back. A new coach trying to establish relationships and patterns deserves some allowance for that disruption. The allowance cannot be unlimited but it should be acknowledged.

Everton itself contained more NUFC attacking endeavour than the scoreline might suggest. Newcastle registered 28 shots against Everton’s 11 and more than twice as many touches in the opposition box. Those figures support the argument that the team continued to compete and create opportunities. They do not establish that Newcastle deserved to win: shot totals alone tell us too little about the quality of chances or control of a match.

The strongest concern is the recurrence of damaging defeats. Losing 4–1 at Leeds, 4–1 at Coventry and now 3–1 at Everton means conceding 11 goals across these three away matches. The clean sheets at Tottenham and Palace are encouraging but the contrast exposes how inconsistent Newcastle’s defensive performance has been.

At Coventry, Newcastle conceded an early penalty, failed to deal with a long throw for the second goal and then conceded twice after pulling themselves back into contention. At Everton, avoidable losses of possession and failures to defend decisive moments again proved costly. My concern is whether Newcastle United have sufficient collective security when something goes wrong.

Matthias Jaissle has emphasised individual mistakes and the need for greater maturity. That is understandable but repeated mistakes eventually become a coaching question too.

Are instructions clear? Are players receiving enough support? Does the approach suit those available? Players remain responsible for their decisions; the manager remains responsible for creating conditions in which good decisions become more likely.

The cup exit also represents a lost opportunity. Newcastle selected a side showing only one change from the Palace victory. This was a serious attempt to progress and it failed. Supporters are entitled to feel disappointed without being accused of impatience.

My assessment is that Matthias Jaissle deserves time, accompanied by clear expectations. Over the coming weeks, Newcastle United need fewer damaging errors, greater resilience after conceding and performances that can be sustained from one match to the next. Another impressive afternoon will encourage; a dependable run will persuade.

There is enough promise to support Matthias Jaissle. There is enough fragility to question him. The fairest position is to do both and judge whether the team is learning from setbacks rather than repeatedly explaining them.

Blockchain Firms Link North Korea to $6.3 Billion Binance Hack 

Blockchain analysis firms Chainalysis and TRM Labs have linked the $6.3 billion Binance hack yesterday to TraderTraitor, a unit associated with North Korea. If confirmed at this scale, the theft would be the largest crypto hack on record. 

Chainalysis puts the total at $6.4 billion in its own assessment. Chainalysis says funds began leaving Binance hot wallets across multiple blockchains around 4:20 p.m. Eastern Time on October 29. 

The firm said the attacker used an operational wallet funded through Tornado Cash to submit signed authorizations that allowed a Binance hot wallet to run attacker-deployed code. 

The attacker then swept assets into addresses under its control. Binance has said its initial investigation points to a compromised engineer’s laptop, which gave an attacker access to a service container and enabled unauthorized withdrawals. 

The exchange said it has found no evidence of a wallet private key leak.

The attacker reportedly tried to outpace freezes by converting stablecoins into ether and other assets that issuers cannot freeze. Chainalysis said the attacker swapped $325 million in stablecoins into $285 million in ETH over roughly 20 minutes, then sent the remaining $175 million to stopover wallets. Freezes began by 5:14 p.m. Eastern, according to the firm.

Market reacts to the hack

Bitcoin dropped toward $76,000 on Friday morning before trading near $77,400. Other crypto followed suit and posted declines of their own. Total market liquidations reached $1.5 billion, with bitcoin accounting for $468 million and ether for $358 million. 

Long positions, which bet on higher prices, bore most of the losses. The sell-off coincided with a separate incident at decentralized betting platform Polymarket, which suspended international trading after hackers drained more than $2.6 million from user accounts.

Binance said Thursday that the security incident affected about $6.3 billion in assets, surpassing the $1.5 billion Bybit hack of 2025, which was attributed to North Korean hackers. Former CEO Changpeng Zhao wrote on X, “It does look like a hack.” Zhao no longer runs the exchange but remains a key shareholder.

Stablecoin issuers moved to contain the fallout. Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said the company froze more than $1.567 billion in USDT and that protecting the industry and its partners is its main priority. 

Circle co-founder and CEO Jeremy Allaire said Circle froze more than $800 million in USDC in coordination with law enforcement. First Digital CEO Vincent Chok said FDUSD stolen in the attack has been frozen. Binance says about $3 billion has been frozen onchain in total.

Binance has enough funds to cover the incident

Binance says it has reserved more than $6.34 billion of company funds to cover the incident. About $5 billion of that reserve is held in the same token assets that were affected, and the company is buying the remainder through market and OTC channels. 

The exchange says frozen and protected assets remain subject to verification and plans to publish an updated Proof of Reserves. 

Reuters, citing a person familiar with the matter, reported that Binance is working with the FBI and the U.S. Secret Service.

Bitcoin may have further downside

Standard Chartered’s global head of digital assets research, Geoffrey Kendrick, wrote in an email to CoinDesk that the negative price action may have some way to go. He said a new base will form after assets are unfrozen and more details of the hack emerge. 

Kendrick argued that the incident could accelerate a shift toward decentralized exchanges. Chainalysis has warned that scammers are impersonating the firm, including those offering fund recovery and recruiting services. 

Victims should treat unsolicited recovery offers with caution and rely on official channels for updates.

At the time of writing, Bitcoin is trading near $76,700.

Rising mortgage rates and persistent affordability constraints weighed on D.R. Horton’s home sales during its fiscal fourth quarter, contributing to year-over-year declines in both closings and new orders.

The Arlington, Texas-based builder closed 22,915 homes during the quarter, down 2% from the prior year period. Home sales revenue declined 2% to $8.4 billion.

Net sales orders fell 7% to 18,619 homes, with the value of those orders declining 9% to $6.6 billion. The cancellation rate was 23%, compared with 20% a year earlier.

On the earnings call, D.R. Horton reported an average sales price on net orders of approximately $357,000, down 2% both sequentially and year over year.  

“Housing market conditions became more challenging during the fourth quarter as rising mortgage rates, affordability constraints, and cautious consumer sentiment continued to pressure new-home demand,” executive chairman David Auld said.

Margins Contract as Incentives Remain Elevated

Home sales gross margin was 19.5% in the quarter, down 120 basis points sequentially. Management attributed 80 basis points of the sequential decline to above-normal litigation costs.  

Home building pre-tax income declined 11% to $891 million, while its pre-tax profit margin was 10.6%. Auld said incentives are expected to remain an important affordability tool heading into the new fiscal year.

“We expect incentives to remain elevated in fiscal 2027, the extent of which will depend on market conditions throughout the year,” he said.

D.R. Horton also pulled back sharply on starts as it worked to align inventory with the slower sales environment. The builder started approximately 13,100 homes during the September quarter, down 45% sequentially, according to the earnings call.

The builder ended Sept. 30 with approximately 28,200 homes in inventory, including 17,700 unsold homes. Of those unsold homes, 8,800 were completed.

Despite the challenging end to the year, D.R. Horton closed 84,202 homes during fiscal 2026, generating $30.6 billion in home sales revenue. Consolidated revenue for the full year totaled $33 billion, with net income attributable to the company of $2.9 billion, or $10.11 per diluted share.  

Looking to Fiscal 2027

D.R. Horton enters fiscal 2027 expecting affordability constraints and elevated mortgage rates to continue influencing buyer demand.

The company expects to close between 79,000 and 84,000 homes in fiscal 2027 and generate consolidated revenue of approximately $30 billion to $32 billion.

Jessica Biel Wants Her Son to Know Why Being a Nepo Baby Isn’t ‘Cool’

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →
Photo Credit: Josh Smith's Great Chat Show / YouTube

A cameo in a movie can carry different meanings. For Jessica Biel, it became a teaching moment. The actress opened up about how her son stepped into a small movie role, and why she refuses to let a famous surname do the heavy lifting. The candid conversation eventually wades into nepo baby territory, even though she doesn’t directly use the phrase.

Jessica Biel has a ‘not cool’ lesson for son about being a nepo baby: ‘We’re not doing that’

Biel shared the story on “Josh Smith’s Great Chat Show,” where she discussed “Matchbox,” her first film in nine years. Her 11-year-old son, Silas, also appears briefly at the end of the movie. According to Biel, he is already a DJ with a comedy streak, and he has said he wants to be an actor.

The offer came from producer Jules Daly, a friend of about 20 years. Biel admitted she hesitated, since the family tries to keep its children away from the spotlight. Still, Silas was heading to Budapest to spend seven weeks with her. “With your kids, you don’t want to squash their dreams,” she said, though she joked that she kind of wanted to squash this one.

Eventually, Biel said she and her husband, Justin Timberlake, felt comfortable because Daly would protect both her and Silas if anything felt off. The acting part was also brief, and that mattered. She explained that other children work hard in acting and theater classes. “You can’t just take a part from that person just because you’re my son,” she said. “That’s not cool. We’re not doing that.”

“The Texas Chainsaw Massacre” actress also said she would not have allowed a big monologue, because Silas would have to earn a role like that. The family, she added, will not hand out opportunities simply because of its name. For Biel and Timberlake, raising sons in a well-known household means these conversations keep evolving.

Life on the film set also delivered its own lesson to the 11-year-old. On his first day, he never worked, and he was upset. She told him, “They will call you in, and you will sit for 12 hours.” Then she reminded him he was being paid. He accepted that, and he worked the next day. Biel also called it the best lesson.

“Matchbox” is based on the Mattel toys and marks Biel’s return to film, where she stars opposite John Cena.

Summer lighting comes easy. Just open the blinds and let the sunshine in. But as the days grow shorter and the nights grow longer, we turn indoors for cozier ways to light a space. Think of the warmth of a glowing window lit from inside, or a snug reading nook warmed by soft lighting. As the seasons shift into autumn, natural daylight gives way to artificial lighting, which becomes the primary way to restore warmth to the home.

Still, the darker months offer a great opportunity to fundamentally alter a space’s essence without buying new furniture, painting the walls, or making permanent changes. That’s where lighting comes in. With the ability to transform a home’s emotional temperature—from cool whites to a warm, sunset-like glow—lighting is key to adapting a home to the changing seasons.

“Layering warm light fills the visual void left by early dusk, softening cold architectural surfaces and mimicking the natural amber warmth of sunset,” says Sherry Ye, chief marketing officer at global smart-lighting brand Govee. “It allows us to reclaim our living spaces as comforting sanctuaries during the darker months.”

Design Each Room Around How You Want It to Feel

Govee’s layered lighting guide identifies three key zones—the living room, bedroom, and home office—and flexible smart lighting solutions to shape mood and adapt each space for the changing seasons.

Photography courtesy of Govee

Cozy Connection in the Living Room

Lighting has the power to transform a space, but it is equally impactful in how it makes you feel. From placement to color, different combinations create different emotional effects. In the living room, the goal is to create a cocoon-like sanctuary that feels warm and comfortable while encouraging socializing and connection. The strategy: layered lighting at varying heights.

Surround the seating area with a cozy glow using the Govee Floor Lamp 3, a double-sided accent lamp that will wash a corner and wall in soft light. Add the Table Lamp on a side table to create another level, then place the Govee TV Backlight 3 behind the screen to soften the contrast between a bright display and a dark wall. Together they bathe the space in rich golden light that doesn’t overpower the room but draws people inward for an intimate—and inviting—atmosphere. Plus, all three can be dimmed and adjusted together from one app, so the scene can soften gradually as evening settles in.

Peace of Mind in the Bedroom

The bedroom calls for a different lighting approach. Instead of focusing on sanctuary and connection, this space prioritizes rest and relaxation. The result is soft, indirect lighting that naturally aligns with the day’s cycles as the body winds down into sleep.

For a low-intensity scheme that maintains a restful atmosphere, try layering the Govee Lantern Floor Lamp and Govee Table Lamp Classic with the soft halo effect of the Govee COB Strip Light 2 Pro. With the floor lamp’s wide reach and the table lamp’s gentle wash, this combination helps calm the mind with balanced, restful lighting before sleep.

Friendly Focus in the Home Office

In the home office, functionality is key. Yet, there is a fine line between a streamlined design and a space that feels sterile. Here, the strategy is balancing clear, functional task lighting with soft, accent lighting.

Anchor the room with the Govee Sky Ceiling Light, whose clear, energizing white light in the 4000K–6000K range brings concentration and visibility. Then soften the edges with the Govee Tree Floor Lamp and Govee Table Lamp Classic as soft, warm accents that keep the room from feeling clinical. “This contrast prevents eye fatigue, keeps the mind alert, and ensures the room feels human and inviting, even during long winter work sessions,” explains Ye.

Shaping Mood Through Color and Placement

Brightness may be one of the defining tools of the lighting toolbox, but color temperature, effects, and placement also significantly shape a room’s mood.

Working both on a physiological and psychological level, the body registers the difference between a cozy reading nook and cool task lighting in the kitchen immediately. In the first, a warm glow invites intimacy and comfort; in the second, crisp white light sharpens focus. “Color temperature sets our biological clock,” Ye says. “Warm, low-Kelvin light (candlelight to warm white) signals safety and rest, while cool white light promotes alertness and focus.”

Visual effects like gradients also tap into the body’s response, mirroring nature’s gradual tonal shifts. Plus, playing with saturation and gradients introduces depth and texture to otherwise static or flat walls. Govee’s advanced color-blending technology is built for exactly this, offering single fixture or light strips with smooth, multi-tone color transitions that bring out the character of natural materials like wood grain, linen, and stone.

Placement is also key. Strategically placed accent lighting can spotlight hanging artwork, plants, backsplashes, or other design moments, while indirect lighting can soften a hard surface or bring out color and depth. For homeowners who want to experiment without the expensive rewiring, flexible smart lighting solutions like Govee’s make room to test placement, along with brightness, color, and atmosphere, until the desired mood takes shape.

Making Seasonal Changes Without Redesigning the Room

It underscores every impactful design moment in a home. “Light is the invisible architecture of a space,” affirms Ye. “You can furnish a room with the finest materials and art, but without considered lighting, those elements remain flat and uninspired. Light dictates how we perceive color, texture, scale, and proportion.”

As the seasons turn and life moves indoors for the colder months, that influence matters more than ever, and resonant lighting becomes essential.

Lighting, fortunately, adapts with the seasons, reenergizing a home without new furniture or a swap of old décor. In fall and winter, that means cozy corners and soft, intimate interiors.
