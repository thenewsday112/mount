Stellar Opens Two-Way Bridge to Ethereum as Institutional RWA Gains Momentum on Network

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

To advance interoperability, Stellar has opened a two-way bridge to Ethereum, integrating its protocol with Ethereum’s mainnet. According to market development released today, Stellar revealed the launch of VIA Labs’ cross-chain messaging system on its mainnet.

Stellar rolls out enhanced two-way Ethereum bridge

Stellar’s move to open the VIA Labs’ multichain bridge on its protocol gives its Soroban smart contract platform direct, two-way communication with Ethereum. The connection now lets smart contracts on both Stellar and Ethereum exchange messages and applications through VIA Labs’ multichain messaging system.

The cross-chain bridge integration matters in several ways. As the announcement states, it lets Stellar developers efficiently move supported applications and assets between networks. It also enables developers to orchestrate contract activities across chains, including building applications around stablecoins and tokenized real-world assets. 

Meanwhile, the multichain bridge addresses fragmentation on Stellar by letting users move assets and interact efficiently with different blockchain environments, including the Ethereum ecosystem.

RWA demand on Stellar heating up

The two-way Ethereum bridge integrates at a time when the RWA narrative continues to gain momentum on Stellar. The network is rapidly emerging as a major force in the race to tokenize RWAs, with billions of dollars flocking into the blockchain.

This is evident in Securitize, the largest tokenization platform, which has integrated the Stellar network to issue and manage its tokenized financial assets. Other dominant global asset managers that recently moved tokenized funds to Stellar include Pontoro, ArchaxEX, among others, underscoring the network’s growing popularity.

By unveiling the groundbreaking two-way Ethereum bridge, Stellar addresses the complex engineering challenge in RWA tokenization. To accurately represent physical commodities, bonds, real estate, and more on the blockchain, Stellar needs not only strong, interoperable smart contracts but also a flawless, tamper-proof data bridge. This is where the two-way Ethereum bridge comes in.

Charles M. Schulz Museum With Kids: A Family Guide To Santa Rosa, CA

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Sabina Graves

When the chance came to visit Santa Rosa, California, and experience the Charles M. Schulz Museum, the timing couldn’t have been more perfect. Our little one is ~deep~ in a Snoopy phase. We’ve been rotating Peanuts classics with The Snoopy Show on Apple TV+ because they’re such great low-stimulation wind-down shows before bed (parents included). So Peanuts Gang vibes were definitely what we wanted out of a family trip, and Santa Rosa delivered: walkable cozy dining, cute cartoon sightings around every corner, and plenty for a little kid to get into.

Getting there was definitely the most stressful part because our once-dozing darling of the skies had grown into a ball of energy bouncing around the cabin. Thankfully, the flight was only an hour long from our home base of Los Angeles. Once we hit the road the rest of the way in a rental car, our drive from San Francisco to Santa Rosa washed away the stress as we road-tripped over the water on the Golden Gate Bridge and into the lush greenery of Sonoma County. Visit Santa Rosa hosted Scary Mommy at the Art House Hotel and put together a family-friendly itinerary built around the Charles M. Schulz Museum and the Children’s Museum of Sonoma County, with some of the town’s best food mixed in.

First up was the Poppy Bank Epicenter, where we got to shake loose energy from all the travel sitting. There’s a toddler play area, an arcade, bowling, and Sky Zone, where if your kid is too restless to sit for food, you can pack it up and head over to an activity to tire them out. Ask us how we know. We’re in the restaurant training era, and she was just not having it. Still, Victory House at Epicenter was a great spot; we got massive burgers with fries, plus kids' dino nuggets to go. After a workout on the awesome 4-person Omni Motion VR screen games, we headed back to the hotel to put her to bed and reheat our food.

Sabina Graves

Here's where Art House really shines for families. Once our little one knocked out, we were able to make use of a full kitchen and sit down at a real dining table to eat like actual adults while still keeping an eye on our snoozing kid. Have you ever eaten cold pizza on a hotel bed in the dark because the baby finally fell asleep? Yeah, then you get why this felt like such a luxury.

The next morning, my husband picked up coffee from Land and Water Coffee, a local roaster with flavorful and sense-awakening brews. It gave us the energy we needed to keep up with our kid, who was raring to go. Our breakfast spot was the Americana, a farm-to-table breakfast nook where we filled up on fresh veggie-packed, cheesy omelets and our daughter happily demolished chocolate chip pancakes with chicken sausage and a milk.

Everything was either walkable or just a short drive away, which meant we could really plan around our kid’s schedule instead of the other way around. Our first big outing of the trip was to The Children’s Museum of Sonoma County, just steps from the Schulz Museum (on the deck for the next day). And it just so happened to be her first-ever children’s museum, so we were just as excited for our precocious kid.

Sabina Graves

Established by a group of mothers in Sonoma, including Lucasfilm luminaries, the children’s museum grew from a traveling trailer of imaginative play and education into a permanent nonprofit space focused on making fun accessible to local families regardless of income. There’s an admission fee, but families who qualify, including WIC and SNAP recipients, can have it waived through the museum’s family access programs.

The level of love and diversity in every experience was so deeply moving. We started my kid in Totopia, a soft-play woodland nook, because we were nervous to let her roam right away. The staff there were great — not just with her, but with us too, gently coaching Mom and Dad to a place where our anxiety could subside. And with so many safe spaces built for creative play, it didn’t take long for all of us to relax.

Next up was the Wild Zone, a purposeful recreational area full of sticks, climbing, and wobbly unstructured natural play where kids can basically FAFO under supervision. So if they lose their balance by maybe climbing something too ambitiously, they learn consequences. Our kiddo needed that lesson! An experimental FAFO space? Genius, truly.

Outside, our daughter fell hard for the bubble and water tables, because what toddler doesn’t yearn for splish-splashing? Take it from us: You’ll want to pack a change of clothes, because we definitely had to change her before going inside. Back indoors, she “flew” a rocket to space, “cooked” in a retro diner, and “doctored” in the Science & Medical Lab. Then it was back to the hotel for naps all around before heading out on foot to our next family adventure.

Before dinner, we hit Noble Folk Ice Cream and Pie Bar (who says you can’t have dessert first), a cute and tasty stop. We shared scoops of their heritage flavors — Strawberry Miso, Butterscotch, and Cornflake Maple — until our daughter spotted the freshly baked chocolate chip cookies and it was game over. My pie-loving husband grabbed a to-go slice of the seasonal maple chocolate walnut. Afterward, we walked over to La Rosa Tequileria & Grille for a fresh coastal seafood dinner and margs (for Mom and Dad) before all three of us passed out, full from the day's activities.

Our final day in Santa Rosa brought us to the main event: the Snoopy and Family exhibit at the Charles M. Schulz Museum. Since we had a flight to catch later that day, we swung in for sandwiches at Mac’s Deli (they’re legendary for a reason) and coffee from Espressioso's Coffee, a Latin-inspired roaster specializing in ethically sourced beans.

After getting in a little playtime in the cafe's toddler corner, we made our way to the museum. As animation fans, we geeked out seeing the comic strips up close and learning the story behind Snoopy’s litter of siblings, the focus of the current exhibit. We especially appreciated that it spotlights Bill Melendez, the director and animator behind the Peanuts TV specials — including the one about Snoopy’s origin — as such an integral part of making Peanuts a staple in pop culture.

It was so cool to see how those relatable Snoopy family moments came to life, from being printed on local paper strips by Schulz to the screen by Melendez. In particular, the "Snoopy's Reunion” episode really captures the spirit of Schulz’s work, and how Snoopy’s backstory only made fans love him more. I didn’t know much about Melendez before this visit (including that the Latino animator was the voice of Snoopy himself), and learning about his mark on animation history genuinely moved me. He and Schulz created a vision of childhood still beloved today.

Now I get to share that with my daughter, who is fully in her cartoon dog era. She was surrounded by puppies everywhere she looked, and the giant plush displays of Snoopy with Spike, Belle, Olaf, and the rest of the Daisy Hill Puppy Farm litter gave me full-blown cuteness aggression. Thankfully, there’s a gift shop stocked with Peanuts merch from around the world.

The museum also has art rooms where you can sit down and draw the Peanuts gang, which our daughter loved. Special tables let you trace classic strips and put your own spin on them; I made one to commemorate my little one’s first visit. We also hammed it up in the photo-op installations, including (yes!) Lucy’s psychiatry booth. Nearby, there’s an ice rink where you can skate just like the gang, plus The Warm Puppy Cafe, a Snoopy-themed comfort-food spot serving pot pies, pizza, chili, and hot cocoa.

The whole trip had the gentle, unhurried pace of an idyllic visit to Charlie Brown’s world. All that was missing was a jazz score by the show’s iconic theme composer, Vince Guaraldi! It was lovely to dig into the history of Peanuts and its timeless take on childhood, which Santa Rosa holds onto with such a charming specificity. We’re already plotting a return trip to see if the Great Pumpkin shows up in the fall, or to catch some Charlie Brown Christmas magic.

Tech Moves: Former Amazon execs depart Warner Bros. Discovery; Amazon leader returns to Microsoft

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Avi Saxena, left, and Tyler Whitworth. (LinkedIn Photos)

— Two former Amazon executives who helped build Discovery’s direct-to-consumer operation in Bellevue, Wash., are stepping down from their leadership roles following the company’s acquisition by Paramount Skydance. They will remain in advisory roles during the transition.

Avi Saxena joined Discovery in 2019 as chief technology officer of its direct-to-consumer business and led its new Bellevue office. He became CTO of Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) following Discovery’s 2022 merger with AT&T’s WarnerMedia business. Paramount Skydance completed its acquisition of WBD on Oct. 6, with the combined company taking the name Skydance Corporation.

Before joining Discovery, Saxena was with Amazon for 19 years over two stints, most recently as vice president of technology for Amazon Marketplace. He also held leadership roles at Microsoft and internet infrastructure startups.

Tyler Whitworth is stepping down as executive vice president and chief product officer for WBD’s streaming business. He led teams working on digital products for HBO Max, discovery+, Eurosport, TNT Sports, Food Network and others. Before joining Discovery, he spent more than seven years at Amazon, leading teams developing products and services for customers and third-party sellers.

Dane Glasgow, Skydance’s chief product officer, announced the departures of Whitworth, Saxena and WBD Chief Information Officer Dave Duvall in a memo shared with GeekWire.

“Much of the strength of WBD’s product and technology organizations reflects their leadership and dedication. Please join me in thanking them and wishing them the very best,” Glasgow wrote.

The company has not responded to GeekWire’s inquiries about the future of the Bellevue office.

Bahadir Onalan. (LinkedIn Photo)

— Bahadir Onalan is returning to Microsoft as a vice president in its CoreAI organization, focused on responsible AI, after nearly 10 years at Amazon. Onalan first joined Microsoft in 2005 and worked on Azure teams focused on search, analytics, business intelligence and security. He left in 2017 as a principal software group engineering manager to join Amazon, where he worked on machine learning initiatives.

“This second tenure feels like a true homecoming,” Onalan wrote. “Our goal goes beyond shipping a platform or a product; we are here to define the global standards for AI safety.”

Naria Santa Lucia. (LinkedIn Photo)

— Naria Santa Lucia is leaving Microsoft after seven years, most recently as a general manager with Microsoft Elevate. She previously served as executive director of the Washington State Opportunity Scholarship.

“I’ll carry the lessons I learned from my time at Microsoft as I continue my personal and professional mission to serve people and communities by teaching, advising nonprofits and mission driven organizations, and spreading joy and human connection,” Santa Lucia wrote.

She did not announce a specific next role.

Leo Ochoa, left, and Aina Abiodun. (LinkedIn Photos)

— VertueLab, a Pacific Northwest nonprofit supporting climate tech entrepreneurs, has named Leo Ochoa executive director, promoting him from managing director for Oregon. He succeeds Aina Abiodun, who is transitioning to chair of the organization’s board after more than three years as executive director.

“Leo brings a unique blend of vision, relationships, and entrepreneurship experience,” the organization said, noting his background as a wildland firefighter, startup founder and patent holder for wearable safety gear.

Abiodun’s accomplishments include co-creating the Seattle Climate Innovation Hub, launching the Opalene Climate Challenge and helping lead the Just Future Climate Labs initiative.

Pornhub is calling on its community to upload safe-for-work content (and get paid for it)

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

It’s time to dust off one of the age-old debates of the internet era: Can a platform known for pornographic videos become a viable home for safe-for-work creator content?

Pornhub is trying to do exactly that. The popular site owned by Aylo (formerly known as MindGeek) has launched a campaign that invites creators to share videos of any type with Pornhub viewers.

In the campaign video, a broken-down car looks like the end of the road for a mother and her son. Luckily, mom explains that she learned how to change a flat by watching how-to content uploaded by pornographic actress Natassia Dreams. The car starts moving again, and viewers are left to consider whether Pornhub can really be an alternative to the likes of YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram.

We’ve asked that question before. Nearly a decade ago, friction between creators and platforms led some musicians and firearms enthusiasts to set up shop on Pornhub. As part of its current campaign, Pornhub is once again positioning itself as a solution for creators who are banned or restricted on other platforms.

There are plenty of reasons why Pornhub’s offer is worth considering. Creators typically go to the platforms that can offer access to new audiences, and porn sites have never been lacking in the scale department. Pornhub claims to reach more than 130 million users each day. Some advertisers have taken advantage of the relaxed guidelines on racy platforms like OnlyFans, and Pornhub has a built-in premium subscription creators can link to their safe-for-work uploads. The recent arrival of Shorties — TikTok-style videos on Pornhub — provide yet another distribution option.

“Pornhub is a cultural access point where creators can build an audience around the full range of who they are,” said Pornhub VP of Brand and Community Alex Kekesi in a statement. “Creators invest time, creativity, and resources into their content. Safe-for-work uploads give them another way to earn from the broader range of what they create, including content they may have published elsewhere, giving content a second life and earn more.”

Clearly, some creators have already taken Pornhub up on its offer. On the platform, the popularity of searches like “gaming,” “fitness,” “cooking,” and “travel” have trended up over the past year.

Can those searches form the foundation of a safe-for-work hub that can rival the one OnlyFans has built? Pornhub clearly thinks so, and it is hoping that its new campaign will convince creators to try a, shall we say, unusual distribution strategy.

It’s an All-Day Pool Day at Lopesan Caoba Lagoon Resort

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

An all-inclusive vacation with kids should make family time easier, not leave parents juggling restaurant reservations, activity schedules, and snack requests every few hours. At Lopesan Caoba Lagoon Resort, Spa & Casino in the Dominican Republic, families can explore a sprawling resort, splash in pools, and enjoy activities for all ages. 

The resort is part of the Lopesan Costa Bávaro Resort, Spa & Casino complex in Punta Cana, a destination known for its white-sand beaches and turquoise Caribbean waters. With family accommodations, supervised kids’ activities, and options for teens who prefer to spend time away from their parents, Lopesan Caoba Lagoon offers a setup that makes multigenerational family trips or vacations with school-age kids easier to plan.

Whether your family wants to spend most of the day in the water or mix pool time with games, dining, and evening entertainment, there are plenty of ways to make the vacation your own.

Room for the Whole Family

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

Coming from a family of five, one of the biggest challenges of a resort vacation is finding a room where everyone can sleep comfortably without feeling like you’re all sharing one oversized hotel room. Lopesan Caoba Lagoon has connecting-room options that give families more space and a little privacy.

The largest family accommodation configuration combines a Resort Junior Suite Connecting room with a Family Junior Suite Connecting room. Together, the rooms offer 1,179 square feet of space for up to seven guests, with one king bed, two queen beds, a sofa bed, and two bathrooms.

That layout is especially useful for families traveling with several children, grandparents, or another family member. Two bathrooms make getting ready for dinner much less chaotic, while connecting rooms let parents stay close to younger children without everyone sharing the same sleeping space.

Before booking, confirm the exact room configuration and occupancy with the resort. Connecting rooms can be a practical alternative to squeezing everyone into a single suite, especially on a multi-night trip.

Kids’ Club and Teen Activities Give Parents a Break

Even on a family vacation, it’s nice to have a little time apart. Lopesan Caoba Lagoon offers a Mini Club for children ages 4 to 12 and a Teen Club for guests ages 13 to 17, giving kids time with peers while parents enjoy downtime.

The clubs operate from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., with meal breaks to give families flexibility to plan around breakfast, lunch, pool time, or dinner. Parents can enjoy a quiet break, book a spa treatment, or simply spend a little time together while the kids have activities of their own.

For families with younger children who aren’t quite ready for a group kids’ club, babysitting is available 24 hours a day with advance arrangements at $20 per child per hour. 

Plenty of Water Time 

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

One of the highlights of Lopesan Caoba Resort is its water features. Families can ride the waves in the wave pool, let little ones splash around in the kiddie waterpark area, splash down on the waterslides, or find a spot in the shade to enjoy one of the lagoon pools. 

My favorite was the cenote swimming pool, something I haven’t seen at other all-inclusive resorts. This tranquil area never got crowded when I visited, offering a respite from the lively swim-up bars and water aerobics sessions. 

For those looking for beach time, the property is adding a beach club, and in the meantime, guests can visit the Lopesan Costa Bávaro Resort beach club. Keep in mind that seaweed can be an issue in the Dominican Republic, depending on when you visit. 

Dining Without the Daily Planning

One of the main advantages of an all-inclusive resort is not having to figure out every meal. You can plan meals around swimming and activities instead of interrupting the day to find an off-property restaurant. The breakfast buffet was massive, offering everything from fresh fruit smoothies to pastries and waffles made to your liking. They even had a whole section for kid-friendly options and Dominican favorites. 

With eight traditional restaurants to choose from, you’ll find something for every taste, from deeply flavorful Indian dishes at Indra to upscale pasta, pizza, and more at Sotto Terra. If you decide to get a babysitter for the night, definitely book a meal at the adults-only steakhouse Brassa Nova. 

Evening Entertainment for the Whole Family

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

Evenings can be a highlight of an all-inclusive vacation, especially when there’s entertainment close to your room. Instead of arranging transportation or trying to keep everyone awake for a late dinner in town, families can enjoy the resort’s evening offerings and return to their rooms when the kids are ready to wind down.

The Boulevard, which connects Lopesan Caoba to Lopesan Costa Bávaro Resort, offers a bowling alley, theater, ice cream shop, café, and casino for adults. We enjoyed live music performances and karaoke at the bars during our stay. 

Getting There

Lopesan Caoba Lagoon

Families can fly into Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ), about 20–30 minutes from Lopesan Caoba Lagoon Resort, depending on traffic. Private airport transfers and transportation through the resort or a third-party provider make getting to the property easy, so you won’t need to rent a car. If it’s busy when you are departing, consider using the airport’s VIP Fast Track service.

13 'Practical Magic' Baby Names Sure To Charm You

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →
Warner Bros.

Pop culture baby names don’t always age well (we’re looking at you, Daenerys), but you certainly boost your odds when you pluck a name from a beloved cult classic. And if a timely sequel just happens to arrive right before your own production does, well, even better! That’s why we’re here for Practical Magic baby names.

Alice Hoffman’s bestselling 1995 book spawned a movie by the same name, and its long-awaited sequel, Practical Magic 2, hit the big screen just ahead of the spooky season. True fans know that the saga of the love-cursed Owens women actually spans four books — and we’ve combed through them to find the best Practical Magic names for your own wee witchy woman. Of course, like any good coven, there are 13.

Sally

Sandra Bullock’s Sally is fiercely protective and somewhat magic-averse… until she isn’t. The name, originally a diminutive for Sarah, means “princess,” so it’s especially fitting for a little girl. Even better, it’s just starting to climb back up the Social Security Administration’s list of popular baby names, meaning you can jump on this name before the spell of uniqueness is broken.

Gillian

Practical Magic’s other protagonist is the free-spirited Gillian. Beautiful and impulsive, she’s Sally’s more-fun little sister whose main fault is having terrible taste in men. But let’s focus on the positive, shall we? Gillian — or Jillian — means “youthful,” making it another apropos choice for a baby. And just like sister Sally, it hits that classic-but-not-common sweet spot. Call it baby name magic!

Bridget

One of the two feisty aunts who raise the young Owens sisters, Bridget is the proper name of Jet and is one of the main protagonists in The Rules of Magic, the second book in the series. Also the name of another literary darling (that’d be Bridget Jones, of course), the Irish name conjures up pluckiness and means “power.”

Jet

Hear me out: Aunt Bridget’s nickname is a standout choice for a baby girl all on its own! I mean, if Bridget means power, what more powerful image is there than… a jet? And as played by absolute screen legend Dianne Wiest, Aunt Jet is nothing if not powerful — in both love and magic.

Frances

Frances, along with the nickname Franny, is another one of those granny-chic names that is so old-fashioned it feels modern again. (Indeed, it reached number 318 on the SSA’s most recent list of baby names — and Stockard Channing’s return to the role might help it keep soaring.) Plus, it’s wise, eccentric Aunt Frances who has one of the book's most enduring lines: “My darling girl, when are you going to realize that being normal is not necessarily a virtue? It rather denotes a lack of courage.”

Antonia

Practical and dutiful, Sally’s oldest daughter (played by Maisie Williams in the sequel) grows up to be a med student. But as a name, Antonia has a flowy, delicate feel that’s, dare we say, ethereal in nature. Sitting outside of the top 1,000 baby names, it’s a vintage name that’s more than ready for a comeback. We think it’s spellbinding.

Kylie

And you thought Kylie Jenner made this name famous! In the Practical Magic series, Kylie Owens is Sally’s youngest daughter (except, oddly, in the sequel, where she’s the elder sister), and she shares her Aunt Gillian’s flaming red hair and rebellious streak. While Kylie as a name is on a bit of a downward trajectory, it’s still a very popular choice, coming in at number 204 on the SSA list.

Maria

The original witch and matriarch of the Owens family, Maria mysteriously arrives in America in 1680 (just in time for the Salem witch trials!) and was feared because of her magical gifts. She cast the spell that ultimately cursed any man who fell in love with an Owens woman. What a legacy! As a name, Maria has enduring popularity and multiple meanings, including “beloved.”

Faith

Faith Owens is Maria’s daughter and a character in Magic Lessons, the first book chronologically in the series but the third written. Separated from her mother, Faith becomes headstrong and practices blood magic. (Can you blame her?) As a name, Faith has long been a popular choice, although it’s not usually associated with witchcraft!

Hannah

In Magic Lessons, Hannah Owens is the one who finds baby Maria, raises her, and teaches her the “nameless arts.” She even gives her the “Grimoire”: a leather-bound book of spells. Hannah means “grace” or “favor.”

Regina

In Practical Magic, Regina is Sally and Gillian’s deceased mother. While her storyline may not be a happy one, the name itself conjures up regal elegance and means “queen” or “lady.” Dare we say, it’s a magical choice.

Susanna

Aunt Frances and Aunt Jet’s mother, Susanna stars in the prequel book The Rules of Magic. You won’t find Susanna anywhere on the SSA’s list of the top 1000 baby names, so it’s a clever pick for anyone who wants a name that’s classic but not common.

April

April Owens is Regina’s mother and Sally and Gillian’s grandmother; she appears in The Rules of Magic. April comes from Latin and means “to open.” It hits that popular-but-not-too-popular sweet spot, ranking at 542 on the most recent SSA baby names list.

If there’s one takeaway from this list, it’s that the baby girl names in the Practical Magic universe are less stereotypically witchy or pop culture-trendy than they are enduringly classic and granny-chic. So tell us: Which name has cast a spell on you?
