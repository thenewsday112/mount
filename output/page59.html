Southwest Premier Card Review: Is The $149 Mid-Range Card Worth It?

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Chase and Southwest have a great portfolio of personal credit cards at three different price points. This is my in-depth review of the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Premier Credit Card, the Southwest personal card with a $149 annual fee (which is neither the highest nor lowest annual fee product).

Now is an especially good time to consider this card, both because it’s offering an elevated welcome bonus, and because it’s your last chance to use credit card welcome bonuses to qualify for Southwest’s Companion Pass benefit under the old system.

Link: Learn more about the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Premier Credit Card

Southwest Premier Card summary & key details for October 2026

Before I get into my Southwest Premier Card review, here’s a broad look at the card and its key features:

  • Southwest Airlines Premier Card annual fee: $149
  • Southwest Airlines Premier Card welcome offer: 85,000 bonus points after you spend $2,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening
  • Southwest Airlines Premier Card earning rate: 3x points on Southwest purchases, 2x points at grocery stores and restaurants on the first $8,000 in combined purchases per anniversary year, 1x points on all other purchases
  • Southwest Airlines Premier Card foreign transaction fee: None
  • Southwest Airlines Premier Card travel benefits with Southwest: First checked bag for free, complimentary Preferred seat within 48 hours of departure, 15% flight promo discount each year, board no later than Group 5, 25% back on inflight purchases, earn 1,500 Tier Qualifying Points toward status for every $5,000 spent on the card (through 2026), get 6,000 points every year on your cardmember anniversary
  • Best for: Occasional Southwest flyers who want airline perks with a mid-range annual fee

In my opinion, here are the pros and cons of the Southwest Airlines Premier Card:

Southwest Premier Card bonus, eligibility, & fees

When applying for credit, cards, the welcome bonus, eligibility rules, and fees, are obviously major factors that potential cardmembers consider. So let’s cover those details for the Southwest Premier Card, as the card has a great bonus, nuanced eligibility requirements, and a relatively manageable annual fee.

Southwest Premier Card welcome bonus

The Southwest Premier Card is offering a limited time welcome bonus where you can earn 85,000 bonus points after you spend $2,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening. This is a limited time, elevated offer, as the most recent base offer was for 55,000 points.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Rapid Rewards points to be worth 1.2 cents each. So to me, the points from this welcome bonus are worth $1,020, making this a solid welcome offer.

Earn lots of Rapid Rewards points with Southwest credit cards

Southwest Premier Card bonus eligibility & rules

When it comes to Southwest credit card eligibility rules, the Southwest Premier Card isn’t available to anyone who currently has a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card, or anyone who has received a welcome bonus on a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card in the past 24 months. However, you are eligible if you have a Southwest Rapid Rewards business credit card.

Beyond the restrictions related to which Southwest cards you have, there are general application rules for Chase cards, including the Chase 5/24 rule, whereby you usually won’t get approved for a Chase credit card if you’ve opened five or more credit card accounts in the past 24 months.

Southwest Premier Card annual fee & authorized user fee

The Southwest Premier Card has a $149 annual fee, and you can add authorized users to the card at no extra cost. Southwest’s personal credit cards have annual fees of $99, $149, and $229, so this is the “middle child” of the Southwest and Chase personal card portfolio.

Southwest Premier Card no foreign transaction fees

The Southwest Premier Card has no foreign transaction fees, so you can use it abroad without worrying about extra charges.

Southwest Premier Card rewards structure

In addition to the benefits, one of the main reasons you’ll want to consider picking up a card is because of its return on spending, so you’re sure it aligns with what you’re looking for. In the case of the Southwest Premier Card, it has a limited number of bonus categories, and spending can also help you toward earning elite status with Rapid Rewards. So let’s cover those details.

Southwest Premier Card points earning

As far as the Southwest Premier Card’s rewards structure goes, the card offers the following:

  • 3x points on Southwest purchases
  • 2x points at grocery stores and restaurants on the first $8,000 in combined purchases per anniversary year
  • 1x points on all other purchases
  • 1,500 Tier Qualifying Points for every $5,000 spent on the card (through 2026)

It’s good that the card has some bonus categories, though there are ultimately more rewarding cards out there for everyday spending. Based on my valuation of 1.2 cents per point, that’s like a 3.6% return on Southwest purchases, a 2.4% return on grocery stores and restaurants (up to the cap), and a 1.2% return on everything else.

Southwest Premier Card status earning

The Southwest Premier Card also lets you earn unlimited Tier Qualifying Points (TQPs) toward Southwest status. Through the end of 2026, you can earn 1,500 TQPs toward A-List status for every $5,000 spent on the card. Starting January 1, 2027, every Southwest credit card will earn 1 TQP per dollar spent, so $35,000 in spending on any of them would get you to Silver status.

A-List is Southwest Airlines’ version of elite status, and offers perks like priority boarding, a 25% points bonus, same-day standby, priority check-in and security lane access, and a dedicated phone number. A-List status ordinarily requires earning 35,000 TQPs in a calendar year, so spending on a Southwest Premier Card could help you earn that. Whether or not that’s worth it depends on how much you’d otherwise fly with Southwest.

Currently, Southwest Rapid Rewards elite status has two levels: A-List and A-List Preferred. Beginning January 1, 2027, though, the program will grow to three tiers, with new names: Silver, Gold, and Platinum. Qualifying for Silver status will require the same 35,000 TQPs needed for A-List, while Gold and Platinum will require 65,000 TQPs and 140,000 TQPs, respectively. I covered these Southwest Rapid Rewards program changes in detail here.

No more Companion Pass qualification through welcome bonuses

This brings me to another big upcoming change to the Southwest program and how cards like the Southwest Premier Card fit into your strategy — and this is a pretty unwelcome one.

One of the best values in travel is the Southwest Airlines Companion Pass. With a Companion Pass, you can have someone travel with you for the entire year (whether on a cash ticket or award ticket) and just pay taxes and fees for them. This is such an incredible deal and basically doubles the value you can get from flying Southwest.

For a long time now, the points earned as part of Southwest credit card welcome bonuses have counted towards the 135,000 qualifying points needed to earn the Companion Pass. But starting January 1, 2027, credit card welcome bonuses will no longer count toward qualification.

This eliminates the easiest way to earn this valuable benefit, and it’s not the only change: referral bonuses, anniversary points, bonus points from spending, and partner spending, won’t count toward the Companion Pass either. In fact, Southwest’s rolling the Companion Pass into its highest elite tier, Platinum, and requiring either 140,000 TQPs or 100 qualifying flights to earn it.

This change isn’t specific to the Southwest Premier Card, as it applies to welcome bonuses across all Southwest credit cards. But the good news is that welcome bonuses earned before the end of 2026 do count toward Companion Pass qualification, so now is your last chance to take advantage before this opportunity ends.

Changes are coming to Southwest Companion Pass qualification

Redeeming points from the Southwest Premier Card

I have a dedicated post that covers how to redeem Southwest Rapid Rewards points for maximum value, but I’ll briefly cover how redemptions work.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, which means that the value you’ll get from your points is tied to the cash cost of Southwest tickets. That said, there are a few different levels, ranging from Basic tickets to Choice Extra fares.

Basic award fares tend to offer the highest redemption value, and in my experience the best-value Southwest point redemptions are usually for international routes to destinations like Costa Rica and Aruba. For instance, I’ve seen Basic awards from Tampa (TPA) to Liberia, Costa Rica (LIR) for 12,000 points and $24.21. The same fare cost $200 in cash, giving you a very strong value of 1.46 cents per point.

One great thing about the Southwest Rapid Rewards program is that there are no change or cancellation fees, and rebooking a fare when the price drops gets you a refund equal to any difference in points. Just note that Basic fares can only be changed by upgrading to a higher fare bundle, so for a cheaper Basic award you’d cancel and rebook rather than change.

Southwest Premier Card benefits & perks

The Southwest Airlines Premier Card offers some benefits that could make the card worth keeping long term. How do the perks compare to Southwest’s lower and higher annual fee cards?

Southwest first checked bag free

The Southwest Premier Card gets the primary cardholder and up to eight additional passengers on the same reservation a first checked bag at no additional cost. Checked bags on Southwest cost $45 each way, so this can save you up to $90 roundtrip on checked bag fees per person. With even a couple of roundtrip flights per year, this benefit can offset the annual fee.

Southwest complimentary Preferred seats

The Southwest Airlines Premier Card lets you pick a Preferred seat on Southwest, if any are available, starting 48 hours before departure. The cost for a Preferred seat can vary from $28 to nearly $50 depending on the flight, so this perk is another potential money-saver.

Southwest early boarding benefits

Another Southwest Premier Card benefit is early boarding. You’ll be able to board with Group 5, or earlier if you have elite status or a fare or seat type that qualifies you for an earlier boarding group. Southwest has eight total boarding groups, so while there are several ahead of Group 5, it’s better than boarding last.

6,000 Rapid Rewards bonus points every year

On your cardmember anniversary every year, you’ll receive 6,000 bonus Rapid Rewards points. Southwest Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, and you can generally expect that you’ll need around 83 Rapid Rewards points for every dollar of airfare you book.

In other words, 6,000 bonus points get you around $72 in Southwest airfare annually. That covers nearly half of the annual fee right there.

15% Southwest flight discount

Each year on your Southwest Premier Card cardmember anniversary, you’ll get a 15% promo code that applies to Southwest flights. Just note that this excludes Basic fares.

25% savings on inflight drinks & Wi-Fi

The Southwest Premier Card offers 25% savings on inflight purchases of Wi-Fi and drinks. Simply pay with your card, and you’ll automatically receive a statement credit for that amount.

Travel & purchase protection

The Southwest Premier Card offers a variety of travel & purchase protection benefits, including:

  • Baggage delay insurance
  • Lost luggage reimbursement
  • Extended warranty protection
  • Purchase protection

You’ll want to check the cardmember agreement for the full terms & conditions associated with these benefits.

Is the Southwest Premier Card worth it?

The Southwest Airlines Premier Card is Southwest’s mid-range card, and the $149 annual fee is in line with many other co-branded airline credit cards. The card is offering a fantastic bonus, which is one good reason to consider this product.

On an ongoing basis, the 6,000-point anniversary bonus can get you around $72 worth of airfare. Whether the Southwest Premier Card is worth it in the long run depends on how much you value the other perks like a free checked bag, and whether you plan to use spending on the credit card to qualify for Southwest elite status.

The Southwest Premier Card provides some nice value

Southwest Rapid Rewards Premier vs. Southwest Rapid Rewards Plus comparison

As I mentioned above, the Southwest Premier Card is the mid-range option in Chase’s lineup of Southwest cards. Here’s a look at how it compares to the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Plus Credit Card (learn more), which is the entry-level option:

  • The Southwest Rapid Rewards Plus Card has a lower annual fee of $99, so $50 less than the Southwest Premier Card
  • The Southwest Plus Card has a slightly lower welcome bonus, of 80,000 bonus points after spending $1,000 within the first three months
  • The Southwest Plus Card doesn’t include the option to spend your way to Southwest elite status through the end of 2026 (starting in 2027, all Southwest cards earn 1 TQP per dollar spent)
  • The Southwest Plus Card offers 3,000 points to cardmembers each year on their anniversary, vs. 6,000 points for the Southwest Premier Card
  • The Southwest Plus Card offers a 10% flight discount each year on your cardmember anniversary, whereas the Southwest Premier Card offers a 15% discount
  • The Southwest Plus Card earns 2x points on Southwest purchases compared to 3x points on the Southwest Premier Card, and it also earns 2x points at gas stations and grocery stores on the first $5,000 in combined purchases per anniversary year (compared to 2x points at grocery stores and restaurants on the first $8,000 in combined purchases per anniversary year on the Premier)

If you don’t care about working toward Southwest status through credit card spending and you want to keep your annual fee to a minimum, the Southwest Plus Card is likely a better option for you.

Southwest Rapid Rewards Premier vs. Southwest Rapid Rewards Priority comparison

On the other end of the spectrum, there’s the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Priority Credit Card, which is the most expensive of the three personal Southwest credit cards. Here’s how it differs from the Southwest Premier Card:

  • The Southwest Priority Card has a higher annual fee of $229, so that’s $80 more per year than the Southwest Premier Card
  • The Southwest Priority Card is offering a slightly higher welcome bonus, of 90,000 bonus points after spending $3,000 within the first three months
  • The Southwest Priority Card gets you complimentary Preferred seating at booking when available, as opposed to within 48 hours of departure when available with the Southwest Premier Card
  • The Southwest Priority Card includes extra legroom seat upgrades (based on availability) as a benefit — something you won’t find on the other two Southwest personal credit cards
  • The Southwest Priority Card earns TQPs toward status at a faster rate than the Southwest Premier Card through the end of 2026: 2,500 TQPs for every $5,000 spent vs. 1,500 (starting in 2027, both cards earn 1 TQP per dollar)
  • The Southwest Priority Card offers a higher anniversary bonus of 7,500 points each cardmember year, compared to 6,000 points on the Premier Card
  • The Southwest Priority Card earns 4x points on Southwest purchases compared to 3x points on the Southwest Premier Card, and it also earns 2x points at gas stations and restaurants with no cap

If you fly Southwest all the time and want as many benefits with the airline as possible, the Southwest Priority Card could be the right choice. It’s also the best pick if you want to spend your way toward status before the end of 2026, since it earns TQPs at the fastest rate of the three cards.

This means that you could earn points at a faster rate through Ultimate Rewards than through Rapid Rewards, and your points will be more flexible (since you can also transfer them to other airlines and hotel programs).

So, should you earn Rapid Rewards points directly with a Southwest Airlines credit card, or with a Chase Ultimate Rewards credit card?

  • If you’re trying to earn Companion Pass or A-List status, then you have to spend on a Southwest card, since Ultimate Rewards points don’t earn TQPs
  • If you don’t care about Companion Pass and just want to earn as many Rapid Rewards points as possible, earn points with Ultimate Rewards, and then transfer them over

American Airlines Heavily Penalizes Solo Travelers: Two Tickets Cost Less Than One

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

American Airlines quoted $496 for one passenger to fly from Miami to Charlotte, but just $155 per person when booking two passengers on the same flight. That means two tickets cost $310 total—$186 less than buying just one. Traveling alone more than tripled the per-person fare. That is one example from a new investigation into higher fares for solo travelers, and it raises an obvious question: why should traveling alone carry such a steep penalty?

American Airlines Wants $496 For One Ticket, But Just $310 For Two

Thrifty Traveler has published a revealing follow-up on airlines charging solo travelers more. His team examined 5,000 nonstop domestic one-way flights, sampling 1,000 each on American Airlines, United Airlines, Delta Air Lines, Alaska Airlines, and Southwest Airlines.

American charged solo passengers more on 33.5% of the flights sampled, compared with 10.6% on United. The researchers found no such pricing on the flights sampled from Delta, Alaska, or Southwest.

The Miami–Charlotte example is particularly striking: $496 for one passenger versus $155 per person when searching for two, both for main cabin economy. Two tickets cost $186 less than one!

American did not respond to the publication’s requests for comment. United declined to comment.

Why Does Adding A Passenger Lower The Price?

Thrifty Traveler traced the pricing to fare rules requiring a traveling companion to qualify for certain cheaper tickets. Search for one passenger and those fares disappear. Airlines have been distinguishing between customers willing to pay more for decades through advance-purchase requirements, minimum stays, and restrictions on changes. But requiring another passenger is a particularly suspect way to accomplish that.

Presumably, the calculation is that someone traveling alone is more likely to be a business traveler with an expense account (all we can do is infer that since AA and UA declined to comment). Even if that assumption works across a large pool of bookings, it does not necessarily say anything about the person actually buying a ticket. I frequently travel alone and still care very much about what I pay!

Traveling Alone Should Not Require An Explanation

A passenger visiting an elderly parent, taking a solo vacation, or flying to meet a spouse already at the destination needs one seat. Why should that person have to produce a companion to unlock a substantially lower fare?

The airline can describe this as a discount for traveling together. From the perspective of someone who needs only one ticket, the result is the same: a higher price for the same transportation. Even when the example is not as egregious as the AA example above, it still seems particularly punitive.

How counterintuitive that a customer entering the correct number of passengers must now wonder whether asking for an additional seat would lower the total bill. We already spend enough time comparing basic economy restrictions, baggage charges, and seat fees. Now checking whether you are being penalized for traveling alone becomes another part of the booking process.

Check The Price For Two Before Booking For One

When shopping for a solo trip, try repeating the search for two passengers. Compare the per-person price for the same flight and fare product.

If an airline wants substantially more money because I am traveling alone, booking Mr. EXTRA/SEAT may make sense…or more likely, give me another reason to book elsewhere.

CONCLUSION

Airlines are entitled to pursue revenue, and I do not expect every seat on a flight to sell for the same amount. But a pricing system that quotes $496 for one ticket and $310 for two deserves scrutiny…maybe not regularity scrutiny, but at least consumer scrutiny. The frustrating lesson is that searching for exactly what you need may conceal the much lower price available to someone bringing a companion…

“In a world whose absurdity appears to be so impenetrable, we simply must reach a greater degree of understanding among men, a greater sincerity.”

What an astrophysicist might have the perspective to eulogize as “the incredibly improbable trip that we’re on” the rest of us might, and often do, experience as simply and maddeningly absurd — so uncontrollable and incomprehensible as to barely make sense. What are we to make of, and do with, the absurdity of life that swarms us daily? Oliver Sacks believed that “the most we can do is to write — intelligently, creatively, evocatively — about what it is like living in the world at this time.” And yet parsing the what-it-is-like can itself drive us to despair. Still, parse we must.

More than a decade before Albert Camus (November 7, 1913–January 4, 1960) became the second-youngest laureate of the Nobel Prize in Literature, awarded him for work that “with clear-sighted earnestness illuminates the problems of the human conscience in our times,” he contemplated the relationship between absurdity and redemption in a 1945 interview by the French journalist Jeanine Delpech, included at the end of his Lyrical and Critical Essays (public library) — the superb posthumous collection that gave us Camus on how to strengthen our character in difficult times and happiness, despair, and the love of life.

Albert Camus

Three years before the interview, twenty-eight-year-old Camus had stunned the world with his revolutionary philosophical essay The Myth of Sisyphus, which begins with one of the most powerful opening sentences in all of literature and explores the paradox of the absurd in life. “I draw from the absurd three consequences, which are my revolt, my freedom, and my passion,” he writes — something that prompted his interviewer to ask whether a philosophy predicated on absurdity might incline people to despair.

Camus — who years earlier had asserted that “there is no love of life without despair of life” — answers:

All I can do is reply on my own behalf, realizing that what I say is relative. Accepting the absurdity of everything around us is one step, a necessary experience: it should not become a dead end. It arouses a revolt that can become fruitful. An analysis of the idea of revolt could help us to discover ideas capable of restoring a relative meaning to existence, although a meaning that would always be in danger.

Speaking at the close of the meaningless brutality of World War II, six years before he formulated his ideas on solidarity and what it really means to be a rebel, Camus considers the only act of courage and rebellion worth undertaking:

In a world whose absurdity appears to be so impenetrable, we simply must reach a greater degree of understanding among men, a greater sincerity. We must achieve this or perish. To do so, certain conditions must be fulfilled: men must be frank (falsehood confuses things), free (communication is impossible with slaves). Finally, they must feel a certain justice around them.

I have often wondered whether Camus had read W.H. Auden’s poem “September 1, 1939,” written in 1940, which includes this searing stanza so kindred to Camus’s sentiment:

All I have is a voice
To undo the folded lie,
The romantic lie in the brain
Of the sensual man-in-the-street
And the lie of Authority
Whose buildings grope the sky:
There is no such thing as the State
And no one exists alone;
Hunger allows no choice
To the citizen or the police;
We must love one another or die.

Complement this particular fragment of Camus’s endlessly rewarding Lyrical and Critical Essays with Albert Einstein on our mightiest counterforce against injustice and Naomi Shihab Nye on choosing kindness over fear, then revisit Camus’s abiding ideas on happiness, unhappiness, and our self-imposed prisons, the most important question of existence, the lacuna between truth and meaning, and the touching letter of gratitude he sent to his boyhood teacher shortly after receiving the Nobel Prize.
