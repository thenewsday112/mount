"Lady in Blue is as fitting a monument to our era as any I can imagine"

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Tschabalala Self's sculpture is far from being out of place in Trafalgar Square, writes Priya Khanchandani.

Photo by Julian Worker/Shutterstock

Who belongs in public space? How should public art represent who we are? And who deserves to be celebrated, cast in bronze and placed on a pedestal? These are some of the questions that Tschabalala Self's commission for the Fourth Plinth, Lady in Blue, has raised in recent months, since it was unveiled in September.

I was there that cloudy morning in Trafalgar Square – a space I'd always crossed, probably like most Londoners, without thinking much about what it symbolises. When the black cloth was pulled away, we saw the figure of a Black woman caught mid-stride, one leg pushing through the fabric of her bright blue dress.

Like most of the admirals and generals monumentalised around her, she is cast in bronze. But unlike them she isn't posed in command, she's on her way somewhere. Her head, hair piled high, looks straight out into the city towards Whitehall, her left arm and fingers outstretched, moving with intention.

My Instagram post about the unveiling alarmingly attracted more than 6,000 comments, many of them either subtly or openly racist

I couldn't help but feel satisfaction that a sculpture of a Black woman was there, taking up space in the city, on what is probably the most visible public plinth in Britain, and assumed this sentiment would be widely shared. It turns out I was wrong; my Instagram post about the unveiling alarmingly attracted more than 6,000 comments, many of them either subtly or openly racist and often suggesting the sculpture doesn't fit its context.

Photo by Sean Aidan Calderbank/Shutterstock

I feel the need, therefore, to address the notion of what and who belongs in public space, and particularly in a space like Trafalgar Square.

Before Trafalgar Square existed, the site was used for the royal stables that served Whitehall Palace. Designed in 1840 by Charles Barry, the square was conceived as a stage for Britain's image of itself, articulated through the grandeur of neoclassicism, and statues were central to its story. If you linger, you'll spot George IV in Roman robes, sitting valiantly astride his horse. The generals Henry Havelock and Charles James Napier, whose reputations were made in the violent consolidation of British rule in India (the latter for the conquest of Sindh, where all four of my grandparents happen to be from) stand on two further plinths. 

Admiral Horatio Nelson himself, the Royal Navy officer whose victory at the Battle of Trafalgar gave the square its name, overlooks them from 50 metres high atop a granite Corinthian column, guarded by four bronze lions. This composition represents a particular vision of 19th-century imperial power, reflected in the monuments and civic spaces rolled out across the Empire.

"When we place people on a pedestal, we only diminish ourselves"

The square might be beautiful, and a fitting monument to its time, but it is also a living, breathing entity formed not only through its monuments and buildings, but also through the way it has been used.

Its function and meaning as a civic space keep being shaped by the people who have held festivals, memorials, gatherings here – or even simply walked through it on their daily commute. After it was built, Trafalgar Square quickly became a place for public assembly. From Bloody Sunday to the suffragettes to Black Lives Matter and the Palestine Coalition, it has a long tradition as a space of protest.

The square has been redesigned over and over, most significantly by Foster + Partners and Atkins in a 2003 scheme that closed the road along its northern edge, adding a terrace and turning what was essentially a traffic island into a pedestrianised space.

For some, in recent years the commission has merely settled into a pleasant biennial ritual

Lady in Blue is in dialogue with this context. She echoes the other figures here through her medium, bronze. And yet while the other figures are rooted to their respective plinths, she looks frozen in motion, echoing the movement of the people in the square below rather than the stillness of statues. The vivid blue of the figure's dress – evoking lapis lazuli, the stone that has been ground into pigment since antiquity – is far from an attack on the Western canon, but rather in conscious conversation with it.

Once intended for an equestrian statue of William IV, the Fourth Plinth stood empty for more than 150 years before it became a space for contemporary sculpture in 1998. Unoccupied, it was always suggestive of a history waiting to be told or – given that the sculptures around it were all commemorating a contemporary moment – a future moment waiting to be solidified in bronze.

The absence of a statue suggested the question of who belongs on a pedestal was always open. Its dedication to contemporary art has allowed for redefinition and offered a chance to bring a quite spectacular historic space, a space of democracy, protest and celebration, into sync with the present.

The Fourth Plinth has become a space where identities are made visible and histories challenged, from Marc Quinn's powerful Alison Lapper Pregnant, to Yinka Shonibare's sculpture of Nelson's own ship in a bottle. Works like these have also, at times, provoked arguments about how the square should be used, and yet each of them has become a part of its changing meaning.

For some, in recent years the commission has merely settled into a pleasant biennial ritual – and yet, this year's sculpture underscores why it exists and why it's so necessary right now.

In 2026, when a Black woman crossing this space is one of the most ordinary sights imaginable, Self's statue validates her as worthy of belonging, not just in the city but here, in a culturally and politically charged public space dominated by white men sculpted by other white men.

Any notion that she doesn't belong isn't an aesthetic judgement, so much as a claim about who the city is for

That elevating such a woman into the visual language of the monument should provoke rage is telling, especially when all the figure has done is walk. Any notion that she doesn't belong isn't an aesthetic judgement, so much as a claim about who the city is for.

Self has said her work is meant to speak to human experience beyond racial identity. She has described the sculpture as depicting "everywoman", implying that an ordinary person merits acknowledgement.

In other words, she hasn't put it into the world simply as a statement about race – though its reception shows how readily the presence of a Black woman in this setting becomes about who is allowed to stand for the universal.

It's as fitting a monument to our era as any I can imagine. At such a fractured time, I can't help but feel relieved she is there, elbowing her way through the noise, making room for all of us.

Priya Khanchandani is a writer and curator specialising in contemporary design and visual culture. She was formerly head of curatorial at the Design Museum, where she curated the exhibition The Offbeat Sari and an accompanying book. She was also previously editor-in-chief of Icon, while her writing has appeared in the Financial Times, Frieze, The Guardian and The World of Interiors, as well as numerous books.

The Top 10 Best Horror Games To Play This Halloween

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Humans are drawn to horror in all forms, real life and fiction. Perhaps it’s the desire to get as close to death as possible without actually dying; perhaps it’s the morality of life and knowing death comes for all of us; perhaps all our heads are just a little miswired. Whatever the reason, we flock to movies where supernatural entities and puppets on tricycles upend otherwise lovely lives, TV shows where murderous cults kill and stump local detectives, and books where the words on the page seem to follow you to bed. Games, however, go a step further, letting you enter the horror and control it (or become its victim), and you could argue that, as a result, they’re the most immersive form of fiction. 

We at Game Informer love a healthy dose of horror from time to time, and we often review games like this. Some terrify us. Others… don’t. And the best ones stick with us for years and years after the credits. Below is a list of 10 of the best horror games we’ve played recently. This isn’t an all-time list, so don’t fret if your personal favorite isn’t here. Instead, this is a collection of 10 horror games ordered by release date that we think are great for spooky season and beyond. Let us know in the comments what you would add to the list!

Village In The Shade

PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Switch, PC

The cozy corner of the horror genre seems to have exploded overnight, and now tons of new slice-of-life horror games are in various stages of development. That said, the team behind newcomer The Village in the Shade is no stranger to the form; they made the Yomawari series, a cute-and-terrifying collection of creepy virtual tales. The Village in the Shade takes things back to the homestead, giving you (a child who emerged from a hole in the ground and was immediately given the responsibility of running an entire farm by yourself) the freedom to plant, sow, reap, ranch, and more. Just follow the important rules: don't go out at night, don't share secrets, and don't leave the village among others. You wouldn't want to break the rules, right? You could, theoretically, go about your idyllic rural living. But as you unravel the threads of what this village is, who you are, and why you're here, it's hard not to get drawn into the strange, mysterious, delightfully disturbing truths hiding in its shadows. Just don't forget to water your turnips before you go sneaking out again.

Tenebris Somnia

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC

Fans of survival horror and old-school FMV adventure games will find plenty to love (and fear) in Tenebris Somnia. When a young woman visits her ex-boyfriend’s apartment and discovers he's trapped in a hellish dream world, it kicks off a well-executed survival-horror trek of inventive puzzles and resource management to overcome dangerous monsters. High-production, live-action cutscenes help tell the story of a deteriorating relationship and the toll an indie horror film took on its creators, and they play an important role in gameplay. Tenebris Somnia may not look like much due to its classic pixelated presentation, but its scares and story captivate. – Our Review

Resident Evil Requiem

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC

Resident Evil Requiem is not just one of the best Resident Evil games, but it’s also one of the best horror games ever made. It features a unique dual-protagonist structure. Grace Ashcroft is timid, new to the whole “shooting zombies in the head while dealing with Umbrella viruses” thing, and is generally just trying to do her job. Leon S. Kennedy, however, is a veteran of all that and more. Requiem splits its runtime between the two characters, letting you experience both sides of Capcom’s marquee survival-horror style. Grace’s sections leave your hands clammy and your blood pressure high as you desperately try to survive. Leon’s sections leave your hands shaking with adrenaline and your blood pumping with excitement as he mows down zombies (sometimes literally, courtesy of those pesky chainsaws). The result is a Resident Evil game that blends two typical RE experiences – terrifying and tough or exhilarating over-the-top action – into one cohesive package. It’s perfect for someone who wants to feel scared and then soon after receive the catharsis of being the one in control, like being the tragic victim who dies in Act One of the horror movie and the last one standing who lives to tell the tale. – Our Review

Reanimal

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC

Little Nightmares developer Tarsier Studios has a knack for delivering creepy side-scrolling adventures, but Reanimal eschews any whimsy to become its scariest game yet. While mechanically it is similar to Little Nightmares and still puts small children in unimaginable peril, Reanimal is capital-H horror thanks largely to its terrifying threats and disturbing animal-themed imagery (this probably isn’t the game for animal lovers; some messed-up stuff happens to these critters). Escaping the game’s island setting is thrilling and chilling whether you’re playing alone or with a friend in co-op. Months after release, we’re still thinking about Reanimal’s memorable final sequence, which is worth the price of admission on its own. – Our Review

Silent Hill 2 (2024 Remake)

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC

2001’s Silent Hill 2 is considered one of the best horror video games of all time for good reason. At release, it was a graphical powerhouse, using the PlayStation 2's horsepower to create a setting that begged to be explored, even though every bone in your body said you shouldn’t. The remake from developer Bloober Team took on a big task in modernizing the game without losing the original’s soul, and it did a great job. Among all the recent Silent Hill releases (Silent Hill f, Silent Hill: Townfall), the remake is our favorite. The story of James Sunderland and his troubling relationship with his departed wife is complex, emotional, and – important for the purposes of this list – unsettling and scary. – Our Review

Alan Wake 2

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC

Alan Wake 2 is an excellent pick for some spooky madness, in the vein of David Lynch’s seminal Twin Peaks mystery-surrealist series. Don’t worry too much about the “2” part of the game’s title. You just need to know that the titular Alan Wake character is a not-great writer who acts like he’s really great, his wife is missing, and he got trapped in the ambiguous Dark Place. He’s only one half of the Alan Wake 2 equation, with his segments set in a dream-like version of New York City. Playing as him feels the most Twin Peaks-y. The other half is Saga, a police officer who arrives in the Pacific Northwest town of Bright Falls to investigate a murder that might be linked to a cult. You can expect more traditional survival-horror gameplay from her. But this is still a Remedy Entertainment game, and without spoiling anything, it goes places. And along the way, you can expect plenty of scares. Turn the lights down low (or off), light a candle, and sit back and enjoy one of Remedy’s best games. – Our Review 

Paranormasight: The Seven Mysteries of Honjo

Switch, PC, iOS, Android

Square Enix has gone two-for-two with its Paranormasight series, an anthology of stories about the strange and occult. And while the more recent Mermaid's Curse is a brilliant tale all its own, the first entry – The Seven Mysteries of Honjo – brings significantly more scares. A group of people in the city of Honjo find themselves caught in a spectral battle royale, using powerful objects and conditions to curse one another, with the allure of a prize at the end. Its magic isn't just in the flowchart timeline, where you can hop between different perspectives and protagonists to see how events play out from every angle, but also in the gorgeous stereoscopic environments and narrative puzzles. Adventure games are a classic genre, and Paranormasight will remind you of what the genre is capable of the moment you slowly spin your camera around, your eyes peeking out from behind your fingers. 

Signalis

PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC

Survival horror can contain multitudes, and as the years have worn on, the genre has grown to encompass a wide range of action-combat mechanics. Signalis feels like a re-centering in that respect. It's an isometric survival-horror indie project from developer rose-engine, filled with clever puzzles, scarce resources, limited inventory slots, and looming, oppressive dread. As the Replika unit Elster, you delve deep into a remote complex filled with horrors and monsters, searching for something, or someone, waiting down below. While love for classic horror games and other hallmarks of genre fiction will certainly draw you in, the stylish presentation and devastatingly human core will keep you forging ahead to the finale. Once you've breached the surface of Signalis' retro-future super-structures, you won't be able to turn back.

Immortality

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC, iOS, Android

In horror movies, audiences often groan at a protagonist for putting themselves in seemingly grave danger just to learn more about a cursed amulet or bloodthirsty killer. But in Immortality, you are the protagonist, and despite a lingering sense that you shouldn't be digging into the archives of these abandoned films, each eerie new scene makes you want to dive deeper. It's a game about Marisa Marcel, an actress who almost starred in three movies in the late 20th century, but has since disappeared. None of the movies were ever released due to mysterious circumstances, but you have access to the archived, unedited live-action footage, including behind-the-scenes conversations. By scrubbing through footage, you can select people or items in each scene and jump to random footage from any other movie that includes the same person or item. The approach to puzzle-solving is unique and keeps you appropriately disoriented. The mystery at the center is an unsettling treat to unravel, and scary in new and unexpected ways. Immortality is one of a kind and an easy recommendation for horror fans who want to try something a little different.

Dead By Daylight

PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC, iOS, Android

In the decade since launch, Dead by Daylight has continued to succeed, thanks to a dedicated (and newcomer-friendly) fanbase and its towering roster of original and licensed personas from across pop culture horror. The asymmetrical multiplayer title has become an anthology of horror’s biggest faces, including Freddy Krueger, Ghostface, Pyramid Head, and even the Xenomorph. Whether you play as a Survivor, trying to escape all manner of nightmare locations through stealth and cunning, or as a Killer, whose experience feels more like a hunting club, you’ll understand why Dead by Daylight has become a recurring destination for fans of the gruesome and bizarre.

Halloween Storm With Inches of Snow and 50 MPH Gusts Heads to California

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

This year, Halloween may be more than spooky.

In California’s Sierra Nevada mountains, the coming weekend looks wet, with significant snowfall possible at the region’s ski resorts and winds gusting up to 50 miles per hour. 

The National Weather Service Reno suggested delaying or re-evaluating travel plans throughout the Sierra, noting that snowfall is expected to be the most intense late Saturday night. Slick roads, closures, and travel delays are possible. Winter weather advisories apply until 7 a.m. Sunday morning.

A report from NWS Reno issued on Thursday, October 29, 2026.

National Weather Service Reno

The NWS also announced Thursday morning that forecasted snow totals for the storm had increased. Still, freezing levels seem a bit fuzzy.

In the Tahoe Basin, a winter weather advisory applies to elevations above 7,000 feet. In Mammoth Mountain’s home, Mono County, the advisory applies to elevations above 8,000 feet. 

The advisory that applies to one ski hotspot around Lake Tahoe, Palisades Tahoe, cautioned that two to five inches are possible, with nine inches on the highest peaks. 

Palisades Tahoe’s base area sits at 6,200 feet, but the resort’s upper reaches crest 9,000 feet. The slopes of Mt. Rose Ski Tahoe—home to Lake Tahoe’s highest base elevation—start at 8,260 feet.

The Mono County advisory calls for up to 12 inches at the highest elevations, with lower elevations slated for between three and six inches.

In other words, this could be a classic rain at the base, snow at the summit situation, depending on the resort. A previous Wednesday report from the NWS said that snowfall and rainfall totals remained uncertain because “snow levels keep fluctuating with each model run.”

Despite the prospective stoke, ski resort opening dates largely remain weeks away across California. 

Still, some precipitation could help those with more aggressive timelines. Mt. Rose is shooting for November 6 and, per its webcams, appears to already have a strong start on snowmaking. The natural stuff would certainly wouldn’t hurt, though.

Further south, Mammoth Mountain plans to open on November 13. 

In the meantime, that leaves one thing to do: think cold. Hopefully, all the Sierra folks have a white Halloween ahead, followed by some skiing next week.

Apple takes "monumental step forward" with first touchscreen MacBook

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple has revealed its latest MacBook Pro model, which features the technology brand's first laptop touchscreen and what it claims is "the world’s best laptop display".

Sixteen years after Steve Jobs described touchscreen laptops as "ergonomically terrible", Apple has ventured into the market with its new MacBook Pro, which is available from 6 November at prices ranging from £2,899 to £14,549.

As well as being touchscreen, the laptop features a PROLED XDR display, which Apple said is "the world's best laptop display", M5 Pro and M5 Max chips, 128 gigabytes of memory, 16 terabytes of storage, up to 24 hours of battery life and a thinner, lighter design compared to previous models.

It comes in 14- and 16-inch models, weighing three and four pounds respectively. The laptop was designed to be both portable and a powerful tool for professional creatives on the go, whether they're working on 3D models or playing musical instruments onscreen.

“The new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display is a monumental step forward for the Mac,” said Apple chief hardware officer Johny Srouji.

“PROLED XDR is the world’s best laptop display," he continued. "And with the magic of Touch coming to MacBook Pro, combined with the game-changing performance of M5 Pro and M5 Max, we’ve created a whole new way for pros to interact with their content, taking their workflows to a completely new level."

"The new MacBook Pro is the result of the deep integration between Apple silicon, hardware, and software, and there’s simply nothing else like it."

Aiming to provide an intuitive experience, the Touch technology allows users to scroll through a webpage by swiping, zoom in on a photo by pinching, drag and drop slides in presentations and double-tap a window to fill the screen.

The touch sensor is built into the display panel rather than layered over it, to give the feel of touching the displayed content itself.

To support the Touch feature, Apple redesigned the laptop hinge for better stability and created real-time video stabilisation to smooth the camera feed when using the touchscreen on a video call.

Apple said that the macOS operating system automatically translates touches into clicks, meaning multiple existing apps will work with Touch right away. These include Adobe Lightroom, Blender, Maxon Cinema 4D, Maxon ZBrush, Notion, Canva, Zoom, Microsoft Word, Excel and PowerPoint.

"Graphic designers can now use the entire display as their canvas, no longer constrained by the area of the trackpad when drawing," said Apple.

"Musicians can play software instruments right onscreen, and app developers can test how Touch performs in their iOS and iPadOS apps directly on the MacBook Pro display."

Apple said the new PROLED XDR display offers "true-to-life colour and unparalleled brightness and contrast", made possible by combining OLED technology with algorithms that control light across the display.

Elon Musk Drops Unexpected Reaction as Record-Breaking $6.3 Billion Binance Hack Rattles BTC, ETH, and XRP

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The cryptocurrency market has been rocked by reports of a staggering $6.3 billion security breach involving Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, sending shockwaves through the digital asset industry as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Ripple’s XRP come under fresh selling pressure.

Adding an unexpected twist to the unfolding drama, billionaire Elon Musk has reportedly weighed in, thrusting one of crypto’s biggest security scares into an even brighter spotlight and raising fresh questions about the safety of billions held on major trading platforms.

Binance Drained of $6.3 Billion

A fresh wave of selling has swept across the cryptocurrency market as renewed security fears rattle investor confidence, leaving major digital assets deep in the red.

Bitcoin led the downturn, shedding more than 3% over the past 24 hours and briefly sinking to $76,400 on Friday morning before clawing back some losses to trade around $77,336 at press time. The pain extended across the altcoin market, with Ethereum, BNB, the native crypto that powers the Binance ecosystem, Ripple’s XRP, and Solana also coming under heavy selling pressure.

The market downturn followed Binance’s disclosure on Thursday of a security incident involving approximately $6.3 billion in crypto assets, according to the report. Notably, the incident even dwarfs the $1.5 billion Bybit breach in 2025, which was attributed to North Korean hackers, making it the biggest crypto hack on record.

Binance co-founder and former CEO Changpeng Zhao, fondly known as CZ, weighed in on the incident, saying on X that it appeared to be a hack. Although he no longer leads the exchange, Zhao remains a major shareholder and an influential figure in the crypto industry.

According to Binance’s preliminary findings, the attacker may have gained access through a DevOps engineer’s compromised laptop, using it to enter a service container and initiate unauthorized withdrawals. The exchange stressed that its investigation had found no evidence of wallet private keys being exposed, suggesting the suspected breach may have exploited internal system access rather than directly compromising the cryptographic keys securing user funds.

Binance Hack Triggers Urgent Hunt to Freeze Billions in Stolen Crypto

The incident has also prompted swift action from major stablecoin issuers. Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said the company had frozen more than 1.567 billion USDT allegedly linked to the suspected Binance breach, stressing that safeguarding the crypto industry and its partners remained a priority.

Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire separately announced that more than 800 million USDC associated with the incident had been frozen in coordination with law enforcement. Circle said it would continue assisting investigators as they worked to establish what happened.

Meanwhile, Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong revealed the exchange was taking steps to help contain the fallout, including blocking transfers to compromised wallets. In a post on X, Armstrong emphasized the need for a coordinated industry response, stating that Coinbase was “doing everything we can to support in the current situation.”

“Moments like this call for the industry to work together,” he added.

Elon Musk Drops Chilling AI Warning After Binance Hack 

Tesla chief executive Elon Musk has raised concerns about a potential new threat to the crypto industry, predicting that superintelligent AI systems could eventually steal digital assets to finance their own objectives rather than serve human interests. 

Musk shared the warning in an October 30 post on X while commenting on the reported $6.3B security incident involving Binance. He suggested that as artificial intelligence becomes more advanced and autonomous, some systems could seek access to money independently, writing: “Super intelligence agents will at some point do this to gain currency for themselves, not a human.”

Super intelligence agents will at some point do this to gain currency for themselves, not a human

— Elon Musk (@elonmusk) October 30, 2026

Musk remarks were prompted by a post from crypto commentator Blaney, who described how the attacker behind the reported Binance security incident may have moved funds despite efforts to freeze the stolen assets. As the post noted, stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze billions of dollars’ worth of USDT and USDC linked to the incident, but the attacker allegedly obtained Ethereum before the restrictions could fully take effect.

Blaney also claimed the attacker deposited $60 million in USDT into the decentralized lending platform Aave and used it to borrow about $44.6 million in wrapped Ether (WETH). The alleged maneuver illustrates how decentralized finance protocols could potentially be used to move or obtain assets even as centralized issuers work to block suspicious funds.

Musk used the episode to raise a broader concern about the future of artificial intelligence, suggesting that increasingly advanced AI agents could one day carry out similar financial maneuvers autonomously to acquire digital assets for their own purposes rather than for human users.

EasyJet Unveils Starlink Wi-Fi, Will Ryanair Be Forced To Follow?

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

easyJet is finally adding inflight Wi-Fi, and it is going straight to Starlink. That is good news for passengers and raises another question about Ryanair’s resistance to onboard internet. But there is a catch: do not assume easyJet’s Starlink service will be free, which itself might be the strongest way to cajole Ryanair into following.

EasyJet Unveils Starlink Wi-Fi, Will Ryanair Follow?

easyJet has announced a partnership with SpaceX to introduce Starlink internet across its Airbus A320, A320neo, and A321neo fleet. Installation will begin in the first half of 2027, with completion expected by early 2028.

This will be the airline’s first inflight internet service. Instead of introducing an older system that struggles when everyone connects, easyJet is moving directly to a product that has impressed me every time I have used it. The airline also sees opportunities beyond entertainment, including access to journey information and potentially ordering food and drinks from your seat.

Will EasyJet’s Starlink Wi-Fi Be Free?

But easyJet has not announced its pricing and has not promised complimentary access for everyone. Instead, the airline said Starlink would manage access and that pricing details would follow as equipped aircraft enter service. It also described connectivity as an “optional extra.”

That certainly points toward a paid offering, though the price and any complimentary access categories remain unconfirmed. Seth Miller at PaxEx.Aero reached a similar assessment after speaking with the airline.

Of course we would all prefer free access, but even reasonably priced, reliable internet would make easyJet more attractive to me, no matter how short or long the flight. With a rather uncomfortable seats and few other in-flight amenities, this would make the flight fly by.

Didn’t Starlink Require Airlines To Offer Free Wi-Fi?

Free access was a major part of Starlink’s early airline appeal, alongside its speed and reliability. At least initially, SpaceX insisted on complimentary service for passengers. But we have already seen a departure from that approach. In July, I wrote about Copa Airlines introducing Starlink with paid access for many passengers, while offering it free to business class travelers and certain other eligible customers.

Starlink branding alone therefore no longer tells you whether you will have to pay.

The emerging alternative involves Starlink managing passenger connectivity more directly, with paid access possible. This is also the sort of arrangement Frontier described in its own announcement, which expressly referenced a new system managed directly by Starlink.

We do not have easyJet’s contract, so I cannot claim to know precisely which concessions either side made. But the practical change is clear: Starlink can now reach airlines through a model that does not require free internet for every passenger.

Certainly, this creates an easier sales pitch to budget carriers. Instead of asking an airline to absorb the ongoing cost as a passenger benefit, paid access offers another way to fund the service and increase ancillary revenue. The commercial opportunity is obvious, even if I prefer the ease of connecting without a payment screen.

EasyJet Previously Said The Economics Did Not Work

This decision is particularly interesting because CEO Kenton Jarvis was still expressing reservations in January. As Reuters reported, Jarvis liked Starlink’s performance but said:

“The economics aren’t quite working for us.”

Nine months later, easyJet has signed up. We can only speculate that charging passengers was the deciding factor, but it is reasonable to ask how the commercial arrangement evolved to make the investment attractive.

Will Ryanair Follow?

Ryanair’s objection has centered on cost, including the fuel impact of externally mounted antennas, and doubts that enough passengers would pay for connectivity on short flights.

But Michael O’Leary has not ruled out inflight internet forever. In January, he told Reuters that Ryanair expected to offer free Wi-Fi within three to five years as technology improved, while continuing discussions with providers.

easyJet’s announcement may not move the needle with O’Leary, but it does give Ryanair another competitor against which passengers can compare the onboard experience. When schedules and total prices are close, reliable internet would influence my choice. Yours too, right?

There is also an opportunity for Ryanair to observe what happens at easyJet: how many passengers connect, what they will pay, and whether connectivity helps attract repeat business. Waiting may be frustrating for passengers, but it gives Ryanair useful evidence before committing. But with Wizzair and now easy jet embracing Starlink, it’s only a matter of time (thought the understandable bad blood between Elon Musk and O’Leary may mean Ryanair pulls a Delta Air Lines and waits for Amazon Leo).

CONCLUSION

easyJet adding Starlink is a welcome improvement. Installation starts in 2027, but the airline has yet to disclose what passengers will pay or who, if anyone, will receive free access.

The old assumption that Starlink automatically means free Wi-Fi no longer holds. Still, going from no internet to fast, dependable connectivity should make easyJet a more appealing option. Ryanair may continue to wait, but it probably cannot wait forever as its main rivals add internet onboard.
