Netflix’s New Crime Docuseries Is Already Its No. 1 Show in America

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Netflix has a new No. 1 show, and it’s a three-part documentary about one of the most notorious figures in the history of the Sinaloa Cartel.

The Fall of El Mayo is currently the No. 1 series on Netflix in the U.S., according to FlixPatrol’s Oct. 31 rankings. The documentary arrived on the streaming service just two days earlier, on Oct. 29.

The series tells the story of Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada García, the co-founder of the Sinaloa Cartel and longtime partner of Joaquín “El Chapo” Guzmán. Unlike El Chapo, who became one of the most recognizable faces of the drug trade, El Mayo spent decades operating largely out of the public eye.

That changed in July 2024, when he was taken into U.S. custody after arriving at a small airport in New Mexico aboard a private plane with Joaquín Guzmán López, El Chapo’s son. Netflix’s documentary examines what happened and the lingering questions surrounding El Mayo’s capture.

The Story Behind El Mayo’s Downfall

The Fall of El Mayo is directed by Darren Foster and Miguel Fernández Flores and uses interviews, previously unseen archival footage and hundreds of court records from Mexico and the United States to reconstruct the events surrounding Zambada’s downfall.

Netflix says the filmmakers spoke with security agents, politicians, journalists, people connected to the cartel and families affected by the violence surrounding it.

The series consists of three episodes, each running roughly an hour, making it a relatively quick binge for viewers interested in true crime and organized crime documentaries. All three episodes were released simultaneously on Oct. 29.

It’s Already Beating Some Major Netflix Shows

The No. 1 ranking puts The Fall of El Mayo ahead of several established Netflix hits.

As of Oct. 31, The One About Matthew Perry is No. 2 on the U.S. series chart, followed by Nobody Wants This, Love Is Blind and Instadocs: Jane Doe v. Cornell.

The documentary’s quick rise is particularly notable because it only debuted Thursday. By Saturday, it had already climbed to the top of Netflix’s U.S. series rankings.

For anyone looking for something new to watch this weekend, The Fall of El Mayo is clearly getting plenty of attention, and its three-episode format makes it an easy series to finish in one sitting.

DC’s Strangest New Show Just Wrapped Filming

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The DC Universe is getting weird.

The People v. Gorilla Grodd, the bizarre Superman spinoff starring Skyler Gisondo as Jimmy Olsen, has officially wrapped filming and is now moving into post-production.

Collider reported that Gisondo confirmed production had finished, while also teasing that the series takes an approach to the DC Universe that audiences haven’t seen before.

That’s fitting for a show whose premise sounds unlike anything else currently coming out of DC.

Jimmy Olsen Is Investigating Gorilla Grodd

The eight-episode HBO Max comedy is structured like a true-crime documentary, with Jimmy Olsen investigating one of Metropolis’ strangest murder cases.

Gorilla Grodd, the superintelligent ape, has been convicted of murdering his father, the King of Gorilla City. Jimmy isn’t convinced the case is that simple, so he reopens the investigation to determine whether Grodd was actually responsible.

The series comes from Tony Yacenda and Dan Perrault, the creators behind American Vandal, which should give viewers a pretty good idea of the mockumentary style they’re going for. Yacenda is directing all eight episodes.

Gisondo is returning as Jimmy after playing the character in James Gunn’s Superman. Jimmy Tatro will play Gorilla Grodd, while Beck Bennett, Mikaela Hoover and Wendell Pierce are also returning as Steve Lombard, Cat Grant and Perry White, respectively.

It Could Be Very Different From Superman

That’s part of what makes the project so interesting.

Rather than simply giving Jimmy Olsen another traditional superhero adventure, The People v. Gorilla Grodd is being presented as a documentary that exists within the DC Universe itself. The filmmakers have described it as something that should feel like an actual Daily Planet production made for the people of Metropolis.

Gisondo previously told Collider that the mockumentary format was one of the things that attracted him to the project. He said he had never seen anything quite like a true-crime mockumentary set inside a superhero world.

And now that filming is finished, DC fans are one step closer to seeing whether that unusual idea actually works.

When Will The People v. Gorilla Grodd Be Released?

There’s still no exact premiere date for the series.

HBO Max ordered the show straight to series in August, with eight half-hour episodes planned. James Gunn and Peter Safran are executive producers alongside Yacenda and Perrault.

For now, The People v. Gorilla Grodd is simply another sign that Gunn’s DC Universe isn’t afraid to take some very unexpected swings.

A true-crime comedy about Jimmy Olsen investigating whether a superpowered gorilla murdered his own father certainly qualifies.

Shopify’s Base USDC Checkout Puts Stablecoins Into Mainstream E-Commerce

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The case study referenced more than $42 million of net settled payments and over 448,000 payments on the underlying Commerce Payments Protocol dashboard as of October 2. Availability, merchant eligibility, foreign exchange terms and processing fees remain important.

Base published a Shopify case study on October 29 describing how Shopify and Coinbase built a Commerce Payments Protocol that lets eligible merchants accept USDC through Shopify Payments. Under the design, customers pay with a dollar stablecoin while merchants may receive payouts in their local currency.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Shopify and Coinbase. It offers a specific basis for assessing Shopify’s Base-based USDC checkout model without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

The case study referenced more than $42 million of net settled payments and over 448,000 payments on the underlying Commerce Payments Protocol dashboard as of October 2. Those figures cover activity tracked across the protocol and must not be misrepresented as Shopify-only sales or Shopify order counts.

The development arrives as digital-asset markets remain sensitive to funding conditions, infrastructure reliability and the gap between promotional claims and operational results. It warrants attention on its own facts, without assuming a particular cryptocurrency must rise or fall in response.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Shopify’s Base-based USDC checkout model establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

The important shift is reducing the visibility of the blockchain itself. Stablecoin acceptance is more likely to spread if stores can activate it without managing smart-contract tooling, operating their own crypto wallet or taking unnecessary exposure to currency fluctuations.

Successful payment systems hide most of their technical complexity. Customers generally care that money arrives, merchants can account for it and transaction costs are predictable. Network choice matters to builders, but it is not usually the feature buyers want to navigate.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

The consumer sees a checkout process; under the hood, a stablecoin transaction reaches the merchant through a protocol that can integrate with ordinary payment and accounting operations. A merchant need not treat holding USDC as its commercial objective if it prefers normal fiat settlement.

Availability, merchant eligibility, foreign exchange terms and processing fees remain important. The report’s zero customer gas-fee figure should not be confused with an absence of merchant fees or other costs.

Automated or stablecoin-based payments are also exposed to ordinary commercial risks: incorrect recipients, operational downtime, liquidity shortages and inconsistent compliance coverage. New rails need dependable safeguards, not just faster transaction confirmation.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Retail adoption should be judged by participating merchants, transaction completion rates, actual purchase demand and the payout experience. Protocol-level figures offer one measure of activity, not a complete view of Shopify consumer acceptance.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

Cadillac Made a Blacked-Out Escalade America Isn’t Getting

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

One of the Few in Japan

American cars aren’t a big deal in Japan, although some models are sold there; the appeal is mostly in niche products like Jeeps or sports cars like the Corvette. Another niche segment that one American car maker competes in is luxury, and you already know that’s Cadillac.

For those who don’t know, America’s definition of premium SUV mobility, the Cadillac Escalade, is actually sold in Japan. More recently, Cadillac Japan announced a special edition that will be sold only in its local market.

Cadillac

Noir Edition

Just launched by General Motors Japan, with its President and CEO, Itaru Wakamatsu, is the special edition, Cadillac Escalade Noir Edition. As we know, Noir means black, and that’s the overall theme of this Escalade, turning it into a stealthy, menacing SUV. Its main exterior detail is the matte black XPEL Stealth PPF, which the brand claims gives it an elegant sheen. Since it’s PPF, the whole thing is scratch-resistant and easy to maintain. Cadillac also added XPEL’s patented PPF on the hood, body sides, and rear tailgate.

Other exterior panels, like the front grille, were finished in gloss black to contrast the flatter shine of the matte panels. The exterior also features a new 22-inch 18-spoke rim design finished in midnight aluminum. Cadillac says the overall exterior finish exudes strength and beauty. Inside, the Escalade continues the blacked-out motif but adds a contrasting wood-grain panel. The interior includes exclusive Noir Edition trims and features, like an exclusive “ornament” on the passenger side. It also has Noir Edition floor mats, with a limited-edition nameplate and silver-colored piping.

Cadillac

Familiar Performance

The Cadillac Escalade Noir Edition is now available in Japanese dealerships for 21,000,000 Japanese Yen ($133,000). It will also be limited to just 20 units, making it truly special.

The Noir Edition remains basically the same in performance and features. The Escalade in Japan still rides on the brand’s Air Ride Adaptive Suspension, Magnetic Ride Control, and GM’s much-loved 6.2-liter V8 engine. The Escalade still has the massive 55-inch curved LED display, AKG in-car surround system, and the tablet-sized command center.
