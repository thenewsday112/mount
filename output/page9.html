What If Your Car's Brakes Fail? Bosch Has a Touchscreen Solution

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

What If Your Electronic Brakes Fail?

Brake-by-wire isn’t some sci-fi fantasy anymore. It’s already here, with plenty of new cars ditching old-school cables and rods for sensors, computers, and electronic gizmos. Instead of a direct link between your foot and the brakes, the car now reads your pedal press and decides how hard to clamp down –  all through electronics.

However, this approach also raises an important question: What happens when the electronics responsible for stopping your car fail?

Bosch thinks it has a solution. The company just filed a patent for a backup braking system that steps in when the main electronics go on vacation. Basically, it’s an extra safety net that could let you bring your car to a stop even if the usual tech throws in the towel.

CarMoses

Bosch’s Backup Plan Includes a Touchscreen Button

First spotted by CarMoses, the patent describes a system that uses three separate control units. The first and second handle the vehicle’s regular braking operations, while the third continuously monitors them for potential failures.

If the third control unit detects that either of the other units has malfunctioned, it can activate an emergency braking function. It can also notify the driver through visual, audible, or haptic warnings.

What’s particularly interesting is how Bosch proposes allowing the driver to request braking. Rather than relying exclusively on a conventional brake pedal, the system could provide an emergency button through the vehicle’s infotainment touchscreen. Another proposed implementation places the control directly on the steering wheel, where it’s easier to reach without taking your hands off the wheel.

You might even get to control how hard you stop – hold the button for more braking, tap for less. Bosch says you could ease into a stop instead of slamming to a halt, which sounds nice in theory.

This makes the concept different from a conventional automatic emergency braking system, which generally intervenes to avoid or mitigate a collision. Bosch’s proposal instead focuses on giving drivers an alternative braking interface when the regular electronic controls become unavailable.

CarMoses

Could This Actually Appear in Production Cars?

With more brands jumping on the brake-by-wire bandwagon, having a backup brain and a new way to hit the brakes could be a smart move if the electronics ever flake out.

But let’s be real: in a panic, hunting for a virtual brake button on a screen sounds like a recipe for chaos. A button on the steering wheel might make more sense, but it’s still a far cry from stomping on a pedal. For now, this is just a patent, not a promise. Like plenty of wild ideas before it, there’s no guarantee it’ll ever make it into your next car.

Five Washington Commanders players key to beating Eagles

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Commanders have had another brutal, draining week in a season full of them. The news that defensive tackle Daron Payne suffered a season-ending foot injury on Friday was a hard to take, especially after so many injuries to big-time players.  

Washington’s dealing with that, formally putting Terry McLaurin on injured reserve, a little dustup with Odafe Oweh talking about coaching and a season essentially on the line heading into Sunday night’s clash with the Philadelphia Eagles.  

I mean, come on.  

The Commanders must find a way to rally and go get a win over Philly that Amik Robertson essentially guaranteed.  

That means the Commanders must look to some key players to raise their game or assume bigger roles and lead Washington to victory. Here are five Commanders (not named Jayden Daniels) to watch in this Week 8 clash.  

WR Antonio Williams 

This one probably falls in the “Well, duh” category. Head coach Dan Quinn made it clear that Antonio Wiliams would see more snaps with McLaurin unavailable. That will be the case, largely working from the slot. They’ll need a big night from the third-round rookie, especially if Treylon Burks – he’s questionable – can’t go. 

Williams showed out against the Eagles in Week 1, with some of his best plays as a pro. Washington needs Williams to be efficient with his targets and create the separation required to get chunk yardage after the catch.  

DL Charles Omenihu 

The Commanders defensive line will play without Payne for the rest of the year. They’re also working without Javon Kinlaw, who was ruled out with a knee injury. A position group that was super deep and impactful against the run especially is now being test.  

Johnny Newton’s working to return off injured reserve, but he was formally ruled out of this game on Saturday afternoon. That puts a lot on Charles Omenihu’s shoulders despite the fact he, too, has been dealing with injury. He has been a solid pass rusher and a good player overall this season, but will probably see tons of snaps on Sunday night. They’ll need to be good ones considering how many defensive linemen are currently out.  

LB Sonny Styles 

The Eagles are using Tanner McKee at quarterback with Jalen Hurts out for the season. The backup has, well, not been great. He could also prove vulnerable to making a game-changing mistake, whether it’s a bad decision or an errant throw.  

Styles is the type of athlete who can capitalize on those types of things. He has been impactful creating turnovers by punching the ball free or picking off a pass, with solid coverage and sure tackling, even if there’s room to grow as a blitzer.  

Styles has been on fire lately, with 26 tackles in his last two games. He’s a true sideline-to-sideline guy, and the Commanders will need him to be a force all over the field on Sunday.  

RG Sam Cosmi 

Putting an offensive lineman on this list isn’t always made for a fun read, but Sam Cosmi is going to be huge in this one. While the Eagles are in shambles, the still feature Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis and Moro Ojomo.  

Cosmi must lead a strong effort from the interior line overall to keep presser off of Daniels and to create some interior rushing lanes to get the ground game going. Cosmi’s one of the best in the business and can control his matchups well. He has been solid working with Josh Conerly on the right side, which should be where most of the designed runs go.  

RB Rachaad White 

White was on this list last time, as the Commanders tried to keep the run game going with Bill Croskey-Merritt on injured reserve. He responded with 27 yards on seven carries, plus four catches for 30 yards and a key touchdown against the 49ers.  

The Commanders need more of a steady rushing presence than that, with the carry total significantly higher even with a by-committee approach involving Austin Ekeler and Jeremy McNichols. White must earn the rock with a solid start to the game as the Commanders try to assert dominance on the ground. If the Eagles pack the middle, then White’s work as a receiver can help keep the Commanders on schedule and into more managable third downs than they normally create.

The Pittsburgh Steelers are back from abroad and looking for a bit of revenge. 

They’ll welcome the AFC North rival Cleveland Browns to Acrisure Stadium for an NFL Week 8 matchup Nov. 1. It will have been a little less than a month since these teams met in Week 4, with Cleveland taking home a 27--24 overtime victory at Huntington Bank Field. 

The winner will take sole possession of the AFC North lead after the Baltimore Ravens fell behind with a loss to the Cincinnati Bengals last week.

Here are USA TODAY’s storylines to watch in what could be one of the most important games of the season for both teams.

Can the Steelers finish drives? 

Pittsburgh has a reasonably healthy 643 yards of offense the past two weeks against Tampa Bay and New Orleans. That production, however, has translated into just two combined touchdowns in regulation. 

Kicker Chris Boswell has been busy with seven field goals to salvage some points. That’s been plenty to escape with wins thanks to the excellence of the Steelers defense that has limited the Buccaneers and Saints to an average of 14.5 points and 252.5 yards per game. 

The Browns were more productive than that back in Week 4, though, even as they rank 28th in scoring offense at 19 points per game and 24th in total offense at 299.4 yards per game. They totaled 372 yards against DC Patrick Graham’s defense, which theoretically applies pressure to quarterback Aaron Rodgers and Co. to keep up.

Contributing to the problem last week was the fact that the Steelers possessed the ball just seven times in the entire afternoon against the Saints in Paris. In those types of ball controlled environments, it’s crucial to come away with more than the Steelers have if they intend to keep their hot 5-2 start going into the bye week. 

Has Rico Dowdle found his footing? 

Jaylen Warren has more than held down the backfield for the Steelers with 855 scrimmage yards in the first seven games. That total ranks fourth in the NFL. 

Dowdle had not found anything close to that level of success with Pittsburgh despite rushing for 1,000 yards in each of the past two seasons. His Week 2 injury against New England cost him some time, and he was ineffective in his three appearances before last week, in which he totaled just 114 yards. 

Against the Saints, he ran the ball well in relief of Warren with nine carries for 41 yards. That’s an average of 4.6 per carry. He then added six catches for 45 yards in the passing game to finish the day with 86 scrimmage yards. 

That’s likely a lot closer to what coach Mike McCarthy and general manager Omar Khan had in mind for him when they gave him a two-year, $12.25 million contract in the offseason. 

If he can maintain that level of production, that will make the Steelers offense that much more dangerous, even when Warren is off the field for a drive or two. 

Can the MLBs continue their momentum?

Payton Wilson and Patrick Queen were everywhere against New Orleans. The former had a game-changing interception to open the second half and the latter nearly had a pick of his own. 

They combined for seven stops, defined by scouting website Pro Football Focus as plays that result in failure for opposing offenses. They missed just two tackles, helping the defense hold New Orleans to 18 rushing yards for the entire day. 

In the passing game, they allowed 39 combined yards on the seven occasions when Saints quarterback Tyler Shough targeted them. 

That’s solid work that was a stark departure from their outing against Cleveland in Week 4. That night, quarterback Deshaun Watson torched them for 77 yards on seven targets, and they missed four total tackles. 

Having witnessed how good this defense can look when these two are playing well, DC Patrick Graham no doubt wants to see improvement from them in this one. Especially considering the struggles the Browns have otherwise faced on offense in 2026.

These Cities Were Just Named the Best Winter Holiday Destinations in the U.S.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

If you’re thinking about getting away this winter, you don’t necessarily have to spend a fortune — and the best destination may not be the one you expect.

WalletHub just released its 2026 ranking of the best winter holiday destinations, comparing 69 of the largest U.S. metropolitan areas based on 37 metrics. The study considered everything from travel costs and hotel prices to attractions, weather, activities and safety.

The destinations were divided into two groups: cold-weather cities for travelers who actually want a winter experience and warm-weather destinations for those looking to escape the cold.

Atlanta Is the Best Cold-Weather Winter Destination

Atlanta took the No. 1 spot among cold-weather destinations, followed by Chicago, St. Louis, New York and Cincinnati.

That may be a surprising result considering Atlanta isn’t exactly known for brutal winter weather. But WalletHub says the city scored particularly well for its attractions, restaurants, shopping centers, spas and food festivals.

It also ranked first among the 32 cold-weather destinations for cold-weather activities and had the lowest travel costs and hassles in the category. WalletHub found that flights to Atlanta from major U.S. cities could cost as little as $245 and require very few connections.

Chicago came in second, thanks in part to its huge number of attractions and winter-friendly activities, while St. Louis ranked third. New York and Cincinnati rounded out the top five.

Las Vegas Takes the Top Spot for Warm Weather

For travelers who would rather skip the snow altogether, Las Vegas was WalletHub’s No. 1 warm-weather winter destination.

San Diego ranked second, followed by Dallas, Austin and Phoenix.

Las Vegas earned its top spot in part because of its enormous number of attractions and warm-weather activities. WalletHub also found that the city can be surprisingly affordable during the winter, with three-star hotel rooms costing as little as $62 per night.

San Diego was the runner-up, scoring particularly well for weather, attractions and warm-weather activities.

How WalletHub Ranked the Cities

WalletHub’s ranking isn’t simply a list of cities based on how much travelers might enjoy visiting them. The company weighted six major categories: travel costs and hassles, local costs, attractions, weather, activities and safety.

The analysis also looked at flight data collected for a Dec. 24 departure and Jan. 2 return, reflecting a typical holiday travel period.

For travelers looking for a winter getaway this year, that makes the list particularly useful because it considers both the fun factor and how much effort and money it takes to actually get there.

And depending on whether you’re hoping for snow or trying desperately to escape it, WalletHub has a clear winner either way: Atlanta for a cold-weather trip and Las Vegas for a warm-weather getaway.

An incredibly simple math error involving standard and metric units doomed a NASA spacecraft by sending it just 35 miles above the Martian surface

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On September 23, 1999, NASA’s Mars Climate Orbiter fired its main engine to enter orbit around Mars. About four minutes later, its signal dropped out as it passed behind the planet, and NASA never heard from it again.

The cause was a mix-up of units. Software on the ground reported thruster data in English units, while navigation software expected metric ones.

As a result, later navigation estimates put the probe within about 35 miles (57 kilometers) of the surface, far below its planned path.

A mission to study Mars

The orbiter launched on December 11, 1998, on a Delta II rocket from Cape Canaveral in Florida.

It was built to study Martian weather and climate, including dust storms and water vapor, according to NASA’s Mars Climate Orbiter archive page.

It would also have relayed communications for a sister mission, Mars Polar Lander, which was due to reach Mars in early December.

NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) managed the mission. The spacecraft team worked in Colorado and the navigation team in California, a JPL release from September 30, 1999, explained.

The mistake crept in as information passed between those two teams.

Developing the spacecraft cost $193.1 million, with another $91.7 million for the launch, according to the archive.

Pounds versus newtons

During its cruise, the probe regularly fired small thrusters to shed momentum that built up in its reaction wheels. Each firing gave it a tiny push that navigators had to account for.

Data on those firings came from a ground software file called “Small Forces.” The interface specification called for newton-seconds, yet the file delivered pound-force seconds, the Mishap Investigation Board report found.

One pound of force equals about 4.45 newtons. The trajectory models therefore underestimated the effect of each firing by that factor.

The flight software on board used metric units and was correct, the report notes. The fault lay on the ground.

The firings also happened 10 to 14 times more often than navigators had anticipated. A single, asymmetric solar array increased the momentum buildup caused by pressure from sunlight.

Usable files reached the navigators in April 1999, but their units were still wrong. From then until arrival, each small miscalculation compounded the last.

Too close to Mars by a wide margin

After a course correction on September 15, the first close pass over Mars was targeted at 140 miles (226 kilometers).

From there, the plan was to use aerobraking, dipping into the upper atmosphere to slow down. The goal was a near-circular mapping orbit about 262 miles (421 kilometers) up.

Tracking over the following week put the pass at 93 to 106 miles (150 to 170 kilometers). Roughly an hour before arrival, the estimate fell to 68 miles (110 kilometers).

The report gives 50 miles (80 kilometers) as the lowest pass the craft could survive. After-the-fact estimates placed the actual pass near 35 miles (57 kilometers).

The engine started at 09:00:46 UTC. Contact was lost at 09:04:52 UTC, 49 seconds earlier than predicted, and did not return during the planned 21-minute blackout.

The orbiter “either was destroyed in the atmosphere or re-entered heliocentric space after leaving Mars’ atmosphere,” the board concluded.

Mars Climate Orbiter (Artist’s Concept). Credit: NASA/JPL. Click image to enlarge.

Warning signs that slipped by

The board pinned the loss on a single root cause. It was “the failure to use metric units in the coding of a ground software file, ‘Small Forces,’ used in trajectory models.”

Eight contributing causes were listed as well. Among them, the navigation team “was not intimately familiar with the attitude operations of the spacecraft.”

Others included weak communication among project groups, thin staffing, gaps in training, and tests that failed to catch the problem.

A fifth course correction was discussed shortly before arrival but never carried out. The operations team was not prepared for such a maneuver.

A daily 180-degree flip, known as “barbecue” mode, had been planned to cancel out momentum buildup. Planners later dropped it without telling the navigators.

The units error itself came to light six days after the loss.

A failure of systems, not people

“People sometimes make errors,” said Dr. Edward Weiler, then NASA’s associate administrator for space science.

“The problem here was not the error, it was the failure of NASA’s systems engineering,” he added. He also faulted “the checks and balances in our processes to detect the error.”

“Our inability to recognize and correct this simple error has had major implications,” said Dr. Edward Stone, then director of JPL.

Contact with the Mars Polar Lander ended on December 3, 1999. It had been headed for terrain near the planet’s south polar cap to dig for water ice.

Investigators found that a false signal most likely shut down the lander’s engines too early.

The information was obtained from the Mars Climate Orbiter Mishap Investigation Board Phase I Report and NASA.

Uniswap Adds Developer Analytics as Swap API Usage Crosses $2B

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: Users of the developer dashboard can examine requests by endpoint, error counts, settled swaps, network-level activity and average trade size. A dashboard does not guarantee trades will settle or remove smart-contract and network risks.

Uniswap Labs launched Developer Analytics on October 28, adding request, success-rate and swap-volume reporting to its developer dashboard. The company said more than 10,000 builders had signed up for the Uniswap Developer Platform and had routed $2 billion in volume through its API.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Uniswap Labs. It offers a specific basis for assessing Uniswap Developer Analytics without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

Users of the developer dashboard can examine requests by endpoint, error counts, settled swaps, network-level activity and average trade size. Tables can be exported to CSV, allowing teams to combine protocol activity with their own application logs and business metrics.

Announcements in crypto often arrive before the data needed to price their longer-term significance. A sensible reading separates what has already been delivered from what still needs commercial adoption, technical validation or regulatory clearance.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Uniswap Developer Analytics establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

The release signals that decentralised trading infrastructure increasingly competes on developer experience, not simply token liquidity. Enterprise integrators need performance evidence and a way to detect when a change damages conversion or execution.

For investors, a protocol upgrade should be distinguished from automatic economic value for a token. Fees, governance, user retention and the distribution of benefits can differ substantially from the headline adoption story.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

A trading interface depends on external quoting and settlement services that can return errors or degrade under load. Observability ties a request to an outcome, giving engineering teams ways to separate user behavior, API reliability and on-chain execution conditions.

A dashboard does not guarantee trades will settle or remove smart-contract and network risks. API request counts and transaction volume also require context: high activity can reflect very different user engagement, transaction sizes or market conditions.

DeFi applications make financial rules executable through code, but code still depends on market inputs, transaction ordering and the availability of liquidity. A smart contract can follow its programmed conditions precisely even when those conditions produce an outcome a user did not expect.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Builders will judge the tool by whether it helps diagnose failed swaps, reconcile transaction history and improve user retention. Actual integrations and service reliability are a more grounded measure than using the signup count alone as a proxy for adoption.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.
