Amorim analyses how Milan were able to overpower Inter: “High pressure and low blocking”

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

By: Oliver Fisher

AC Milan head coach Ruben Amorim refused to be drawn into discussions about the Scudetto, even if he does see improvements from his side.

Milan were in dreamland after 45 minutes of their derby clash against Inter, going in 3-1 up at the break thanks to goals from Christian Pulisic, Adrien Rabiot and Diego Moreira. As it would turn out, that would be enough to win the bragging rights.

It marked a fourth straight win for Milan in the derby and it reduces the gap to the city rivals back down to four points. While there is still work to be done for the Rossoneri, it feels like a big step forward mentally for the squad.

Amorim spoke to Sky after the full-time whistle to reflect on what was a memorable night for him as a head coach and for the team, with his words relayed by MilanNews.

Is this the most important victory of the start of the season?

“It’s important. The atmosphere is fantastic, and you can tell after a match like that, especially on a Wednesday like this. It’s clear, even more so after the defeat against Bologna, we wanted to celebrate even more.

“I’m really proud of what my players did, and with so little rest between matches, and especially once again after Bologna, they really fought for the entire match. I’m really proud.”

A nearly perfect team: do you have anything to complain about? You seemed calmer today…

“I try to read and interpret what the team tells me: from the first minute I had the feeling that we were ready to be competitive. The team has always been focused and calm, and so I always had this confidence.”

Photo by AC Milan

Is AC Milan a different Milan with Modric, Rabiot, and Pulisic?

“It’s not just those three players, I think it’s the whole atmosphere, the team’s focus. I like the way everyone plays and expresses themselves. For example, I’d like to talk a little about Pavlović and De Vrij.

“They performed well in every game, especially against such a strong team, even in the penalty area. And then, obviously, players like Rabiot, Pulisic, Moreira… they all did very well. All together, I have to say, we were a better team.”

Why Gila on the right and De Winter in the centre?

“Looking at Inter’s stats, we didn’t know if Dimarco would play. They play and cross a lot from the wings and come to create a lot of density in the box, so I think Pavlović and De Winter are the best for crossing.

“That’s the idea: to match our defenders with their attackers, so everyone can play in slightly different defensive roles.”

How did you manage not to concede anything to Inter in the second half?

“I think the lines were closer together; you could see that throughout the match in terms of high pressure and low blocking. The lines were always close together, so the distances were shorter, which obviously also allowed us to run less and stay fresher throughout the game.

“We were very aggressive on the outside, intercepting many crosses before they were launched, which is very difficult against a team like Inter. We defended well by pressing high up, but we also defended well on the sides. We were more aggressive than we usually are.”

Is the Scudetto possible?

“What Cardinale was saying is that the objective is the Scudetto, but it’s important to win these games to make the fans understand that things won’t change in a month, two months, or a year.

“We have to go through this process: you can’t just hire a new coach, replace all the players, and have a new squad for each new coach; that takes time. Then we’ll need magical nights and evenings, and obviously we’ll need to get through difficulties like Wednesday’s and the joy of tonight.

“I want to say that the situation won’t change overnight; it’s not possible. Is the Scudetto possible? I don’t know when, but we still have to be very honest with the fans, and I know that people aren’t stupid; they understand and know the growth this team and this club are in.”

1000+ Installs Later: DRY for your Storage with Filament

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A year ago, I released Dedupler - Laravel package that prevents duplicate file storage using SHA-1 hashing. The idea was simple: don't repeat yourself (DRY) shouldn't just apply to code; it should apply to your storage too. Why store the same 10MB PDF five times just because it's attached to five different models?

To my surprise, the package crossed 1,000+ installs. It turns out that dealing with storage bloat and slow backups is a pain point many of us share.

However, the relevance of deduplication isn't fading - it's becoming more critical. With SSD and cloud storage prices surging over the past year, every wasted gigabyte has a real cost.

But I noticed a barrier: some developers wanted to use the package but found the setup a bit too manual. They just wanted a simple file upload field in their admin panel that magically doesn't create duplicates.

So, I built a solution for the Laravel community's favorite admin panel.

🚀 Introducing filament-dedupler-uploader
It's a lightweight Filament plugin that gives you a ready-to-use UI for Dedupler. It brings the DRY principle to your Filament forms.

Instead of writing custom logic, you can now just use the component:

use Maxkhim\FilamentDeduplerUploader\Forms\Components\DeduplerFileUploader; DeduplerFileUploader::make('attachment') ->multiple()

What happens under the hood?

The user uploads a file.

The plugin checks the SHA-1 hash via Dedupler.

If it exists wherever else in Storage, it links the existing physical file to your model.

If it doesn't, it stores it once and links it.

No duplicates. Cleaner database. Smaller cloud bill.

If you are running a SaaS, a CMS, or any Filament app with file uploads, give it a try.

One POST read cross-tenant secrets

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A single POST to management.azure.com, with a request path full of %2e%2e sequences, returned the plaintext value of a secret stored in a different tenant's Key Vault. The request went through a connection the attacker owned, but the token that actually read the secret belonged to the victim. That is one of five critical cross-tenant elevation-of-privilege bugs a researcher found in Azure API Connections, the layer underneath Azure Logic Apps connectors. The set paid out $200,000 and was presented at Blue Hat Asia 2026.

How API Connections work

An API Connection stores credentials for a backend: a Key Vault, an Azure SQL database, Jira, Salesforce, anything a Logic App connector can talk to. When a Logic App runs an action, it calls a shared Azure API Management (APIM) instance. APIM checks the connector's OpenAPI definition to confirm the action is valid, swaps the caller's token for the token configured on the connection, and makes the real call to the backend with that second token.

The list of backends is effectively unbounded, and that is where the trouble starts. Whatever credential sits on a connection is usually the full-privilege one. For OAuth connections it is the token of whoever created the connection. For API key connections, the researcher's bet is that almost nobody scopes the key down. Microsoft's own connectors don't.

The attack surface opens because you can reach APIM directly through the Azure Resource Manager REST API, via the DynamicInvoke endpoint on a connection resource. ARM, as the resource manager, has rights to every connection regardless of which one the request names. Make the request resolve to a connection you don't own and you get the victim's configured token applied against their backend.

Five bugs, one root cause

The first bug was plain path traversal through DynamicInvoke. Microsoft marked it fixed, but the fix restricted the allowed paths rather than addressing the token reuse underneath.

The researcher then provisioned a Standard Logic App, which gets a dedicated host, SSHed in, and pulled the relevant ARM resource-type code off the machine. The code showed the fix, and also showed that DynamicInvoke was one of four sibling functions along the same path. The others, including DynamicList, were undocumented and reachable. Since the path blacklist covered only DynamicInvoke, DynamicList still carried the traversal. A crafted DynamicList call ran an arbitrary INSERT against a victim's Azure SQL server through their connection.

The researcher reported DynamicList on its own, expecting a targeted fix that would leave the remaining siblings to report later. Microsoft instead blocked all of those endpoints and added no other mitigation. If they ever come back, the traversal comes back with them.

That closed the ARM route, so the next way in was the Logic Apps themselves. Consumption Logic Apps, which are co-located, create Version 1 connections; Standard Logic Apps, which run on a dedicated host, create Version 2. A Standard app authorizes itself to a backend using its managed identity. A Consumption app has no identity to authorize, which means every Consumption Logic App has implicit access to every Version 1 connection. The only thing in the way is input validation.

The workflow GUI won't let you point an action at a connection you don't own; the runtime validates ownership. The code view and the API accept more parameters than the GUI shows, though, and one of them is host.api.RuntimeUrl. After validating the connection named in host.connection, the runtime lets you override the runtime URL with something else. As long as the host ends in azure-apihub.net, it still attaches the required Authorization header. Point it at a victim's connection and the victim's token goes along.

Microsoft's fix required the runtime URL to match the connection's expected endpoint exactly. That is a reasonable rule, except the check runs before the path is normalized, so the validator sees a URL pointing at your connection while APIM resolves it to the victim's. The same cross-tenant read came back as a separate bug. When Microsoft fixed that normalization gap, their change stopped validating the original RuntimeUrl path, which reopened the first RuntimeUrl attack.

What to take from it

Every one of these is the same underlying issue reached a different way. By the researcher's own guess the connection's stored credential rarely gets scoped down, and the only barrier between an attacker and that credential is input validation that has to be perfect across path traversal, undocumented sibling endpoints, parameter overrides, and validation ordering. Four rounds of fixes all targeted the symptom.

The one control in your hands is the caveat the researcher keeps flagging. Scope the credential on every API Connection to the least privilege the connector needs. An OAuth connection inherits the creator's access, so build connections with a dedicated, narrowly permissioned identity rather than an admin's account. An API key connection is only as dangerous as the key, so provision keys that can do one thing. None of that stops the traversal, but it bounds what a successful traversal gets.

AC Milan recorded a huge win in the derby against Inter, scoring three times in the first half to secure a 3-1 win.

It did not take long for Milan to go in front, with Christian Pulisic heading in a cross from Diego Moreira. Then, just a few minutes later, Adrien Rabiot was on hand to fire in a corner first time and make it a two-goal lead.

Alessandro Bastoni scored from a corner to make it a one-goal lead once again, but then Moreira’s free-kick re-established the two-goal lead and that would be enough to see the Rossoneri through to the end of the game.

It marked a fourth straight win for Milan in the derby and it reduces the gap to the city rivals back down to four points.

First half: A red-hot start

Ruben Amorim chose to make six changes from the team that lost to Bologna on Wednesday night, with Mario Gila, Sankhoun Diawara, Luka Modric, Adrien Rabiot, Diego Moreira and Christian Pulisic all getting the nod.

Inside four minutes, Milan took the lead and it was the derby talisman Christian Pulisic who struck once again. It was quite a simple move down the right side in the end, with Diego Moreira checking back inside onto his left foot and whipped in a simply perfect cross for Pulisic to nod in from a few yards out.

Then, just a few minutes later, Milan doubled their advantage from a set-piece after another good spell of pressure. A low delivery from Moreira found the run of Rabiot towards the near post, who somehow managed to divert it in via the far post to make it 2-0.

Midway through the first half, Inter got themselves back into the game through a dead ball of their own. A corner was flicked on at the near post towards Bastoni, who was on hand to nod in from a few yards with the Milan defenders rooted.

Inter assumed the ascendancy after the goal, and they twice threatened in quick succession shortly after their first goal. Calhanoglu had a shot tipped over from the edge of the box, then another flicked header from a corner drew a desperate reaction save from Maignan.

Milan continued to give their city rivals opportunities to try and make it a brand new game. A silly foul by Pavlovic on Spence drew a free-kick just off the right edge of the box, though the eventual shot from Diouf was grasped by Maignan.

Just over 10 minutes before the break, Inter had another chance to draw themselves level. A cross from the left was not cut out by Koni De Winter, and the ball fells to Spence who fired with his left foot, but the Frenchman makes a brilliant reaction save to deflect the shot out for a corner.

Just two minutes before the break, Milan restored their two-goal advantage thanks to a quite simply beautiful free-kick from Diego Moreira. After getting two assists to his name already on the night, he turned scorer with a fantastic attempt that was up and over the wall, dipping just enough to land inside the near right post.

Second half: Guiding it home

Ten minutes into the second half, Inter had the first big opening and it fell to their recent danger man Carlos Augusto. A cross to the far post wasn’t tracked by anyone and the wing-back ghosted in at the far post, heading it wide from about a yard off the ground.

With 25 minutes left, a corner this time for Milan: Modric’s cross from the left finds Rabiot at the near post, who tries to head it in, but his connection was poor and the ball went straight out.

In the 75th minute, it looked like the game was going to be on once again when the referee gave a penalty for what he adjudged to be a handball on Mario Gila. The replays show that the cross struck his midriff, and therefore the penalty was overturned to be a corner.

With the game settled into a rhythm of attack versus defence, Amorim chose to make a double change with eight minutes to go. Pulisic and Diawara were the ones who came off, with Alphadjo Cisse and Davide Bartesaghi the ones to come on.

In the penultimate minute of added time, a double sub came from Amorim. Ramos and Moreira came off, Filippo Terracciano and Omari Hutchinson came on.
