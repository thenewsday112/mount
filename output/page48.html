Binance Details $6.3B Hot-Wallet Breach as Exchange Pledges to Cover Losses

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TL;DR: The company reported approximately $3 billion of affected assets frozen on-chain through coordination with issuers, investigators and other industry participants. Frozen tokens are not the same as recovered cash, and assets immobilized on-chain still require legal and technical steps to return.

Binance said unauthorized withdrawals began after its global hot-wallet infrastructure was compromised on October 29 at approximately 20:05 UTC. In its October 30 update, the exchange put the value of affected tokens at about $6.3 billion and said withdrawals were temporarily suspended pending additional security checks.

The announcement and the numbers

The most concrete starting point is the dated disclosure from Binance. It offers a specific basis for assessing Binance’s $6.3 billion hot-wallet breach without turning the story into unsupported price speculation.

The company reported approximately $3 billion of affected assets frozen on-chain through coordination with issuers, investigators and other industry participants. It said more than $6.34 billion of company resources were earmarked to absorb losses, with roughly $5 billion already held in the relevant tokens while it sourced the balance.

The development arrives as digital-asset markets remain sensitive to funding conditions, infrastructure reliability and the gap between promotional claims and operational results. It warrants attention on its own facts, without assuming a particular cryptocurrency must rise or fall in response.

Why the development matters

The question for market participants is what Binance’s $6.3 billion hot-wallet breach establishes today and what remains uncertain. An immediate reaction is not the same as evidence of a durable economic outcome.

For traders and institutions, the issue is not confined to the headline loss. A platform can promise balance-sheet compensation while users remain exposed to withdrawal interruptions, unsettled orders and uncertainty about the integrity of infrastructure on which they rely.

Users face two different forms of risk during a security incident. Asset losses are the direct financial concern, while withdrawals, service interruptions and unclear communications can make it difficult to assess or manage exposure. Investigations are strongest when confirmed technical evidence is separated from hypotheses about attackers.

Investors also need to separate changes in infrastructure from changes in token demand. Revenue may accrue to a company, a protocol treasury or a service provider without necessarily creating a one-for-one benefit for holders of a related token. Market impact depends on adoption, competition and the design of whatever fees or incentives are involved.

Risks the headline does not resolve

None of this removes the need for independent assessment. The published evidence identifies a development, while its risk and long-term economics are still open to examination.

Binance initially cited a potentially compromised laptop, but said subsequent work identified further points of intrusion. It stated that investigators had not found evidence that wallet private keys were leaked. That distinction leaves the precise authorization and infrastructure failure unresolved until a verified technical post-mortem is published.

Frozen tokens are not the same as recovered cash, and assets immobilized on-chain still require legal and technical steps to return. The exchange’s reimbursement commitment is an announcement rather than independently audited proof that every affected account has already been restored.

Security controls have to account for both malicious transactions and legitimate users who need access. Freezing, recovery and reimbursement mechanisms are not interchangeable: a frozen asset may be immobilized, a reimbursed user may be made whole, and a forensic investigation may still be incomplete.

The milestones to watch

The immediate checklist is relatively straightforward: look for verifiable implementation details and avoid interpreting a stated target as an outcome that has already occurred.

Binance said it was targeting a phased restart of withdrawals after security validation, planned an updated proof-of-reserves report by November 2 and expected to disclose further verified technical findings. Those milestones, rather than speculative attacker attribution, are the tests for the recovery process.

A durable signal would be consistent follow-through: actual usage, transparent reporting and a credible explanation of risk. Short-term token-price moves can be driven by macroeconomic conditions or leverage and may say little about whether the product or initiative succeeds over months and years.

From a market perspective, the first task is to separate the scale of the announcement from the scale of economic activity that has actually occurred. A company may report a large addressable market, an institutional name or a future capability, but those are not equivalent to settled transactions or recurring fee income. This is why the primary disclosure is a better starting point than a trading narrative attached to it.

A Cracked Oil Pan Killed One of Porsche's Most Reliable Flat-Six Engines

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Porsche MA1.04

In the bible of engine reliability, you need a couple of things: A proven design and architecture, proper usage cycles and care, and religious maintenance and service intervals. The Porsche MA1.04 is part of a series of internal combustion engine families from Stuttgart that is regarded as some of the brand’s most reliable powertrains it has ever produced, so for one to come into the shop for a teardown is somewhat of an event.

Even some more modern Porsches aren’t exempt from the occasional engine problem here and there. Porsche’s very quick to revise their design once a flaw is identified, but somehow, the MA1 series just got it mostly right. While not perfect, the flat-six still has a few failure points and maintenance-intensive areas that owners should know about, but none of those failure points appeared to be the culprit when this motor decided to blow.

So that’s why it blew up.

Right off the bat, I Do Cars found the root cause. There’s a crack in the oil pan. So the engine ran without oil once the level dipped to dangerous levels, and it soon became a coffee table.

The damage done was reportedly $10,000 at the time for a new motor without labor. The engine was graciously donated by a fan of I Do Cars after being sat for a while. While the damage to the owner’s wallet is documented, the full extent of the damage to the motor and why it was replaced was only revealed towards the end of the video.

In the middle of the teardown, I Do Cars did notice a few things that were not ideal for a motor, like some shavings and fragments in the combustion chamber. That was just a teaser of things to come, however.

I Do Cars - YouTube

What Blew Up?

So after getting all the nuts and bolts out of the assembly, the engine comes apart. It’s interesting to note that the MA1 splits apart right down the middle. Once the flat six turned into two inline-threes, we get to see what went wrong. Up until this point, everything was peak German precision. However, the deeper I Do Cars dug, the more heartbreaking it ended up being.

All the rods and pistons were intact with acceptable wear, and even the rod bearings were good, but they have seen better days. That all changes, however, when the camera pointed to the main bearings. One bearing welded itself to the crankshaft, while the rest looked like it had been mangled.

That’s not the worst part, though. On one half of the block, we can see a nasty score at the crankcase. Basically, chunks of aluminum are just sitting there in a crevice. This is a bad case of a spun bearing, and it was enough to basically stop the engine dead in its tracks.

The fact that this was all caused by a hit to the oil pan makes this even more heartbreaking. Now, yes, some parts from this motor can still be reused by resourceful builders, but the block itself might be out of commission. An extremely skilled machinist might be able to repair it, but it could cost more to repair than to replace as a whole unit. Maybe the block is destined to be turned into a coffee table after all.

Arsenal transfer news: Gunners explore loan move for Real Madrid striker Endrick

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Arsenal are keeping their options open regarding a January move for Real Madrid striker Endrick, as Mikel Arteta looks to strengthen his attacking options.

The Gunners have been in contact about a loan move for the Brazilian. There is also talk of a permanent move. However, Real Madrid are reluctant to let him leave and feel he has a big future ahead at the club.

Endrick has struggled to earn consistent game time at the Bernabeu.

While he is only 20 years old and has plenty of time left to make his mark at the top level, a new transfer away from Madrid could give him a more consistent role.

Arsenal see opportunity as Endrick searches for minutes

Arsenal are among the clubs keeping an eye on Endrick ahead of the January transfer window, reports TEAMtalk.

The forward was loaned to Lyon for the second half of last season to get more regular game time.

However, his representatives are said to be open to looking for another move if things do not improve in Madrid.

It seems Arsenal are interested in signing Endrick on loan with a view to making the move permanent. This will give them time to see how he settles into the Premier League.

However, Real Madrid may be in a strong position. They are still loyal to the young player and would prefer to keep him at the Spanish club.

Arsenal will rely on Madrid being more willing to let him go.

Endrick could give Arteta another attacking option

The reported interest from Arsenal comes as Arteta looks to strengthen his attacking options.

Endrick would provide variety to an attacking line-up which has been under pressure of late. His age and potential make a loan deal with an option to make the move permanent an attractive option.

There is also positivity from his previous stint with Lyon. He has already been on a loan move from Madrid and has demonstrated that he can make progress when getting regular game time.

Arsenal have previously signed young talents from Real Madrid.

Martin Odegaard came to north London as a young player after failing to make an impact in the first team at Madrid. He then became a key player for Arteta, taking on the role of captain.

Endrick will be hoping to follow a similar trajectory.

Real Madrid stance could decide January transfer

There is no suggestion that any deal is near despite Arsenal’s interest.

Endrick remains an important part of Real Madrid’s long-term plans. His representatives are thought to be looking at other options to help his career develop.

This loan deal would provide a solution for all parties. Arsenal would have another attacker to bring in, Endrick would play more regularly and Madrid would still have an interest in his future.

It will also depend on how willing the Spanish giants are to move their stance before January.

For now, Arsenal are in contact with Endrick as they consider a move. The Brazilian’s future may soon become a topic of interest ahead of the winter transfer window.

G20 foreign ministers in Atlanta meeting head to an ending without consensus on key issues

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

ATLANTA (AP) — Top diplomats from the Group of 20 largest world economies are wrapping up two days of talks Saturday without reaching consensus on several critical global security issues after divisions, notably over Russia’s war with Ukraine, overshadowed the start of the Atlanta meeting.

As a result, the group will not release a joint communique but will rather rely on a chairman’s statement summarizing the talks, which Secretary of State Marco Rubio will release at the conclusion, according to diplomats who attended.

Nonetheless, Rubio said the first day of the session was “very productive,” and he was hosting talks with his counterparts from Australia, India and Japan on Saturday before leading the final G20 event. That discussion on supply chains will examine freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red, Black and South China seas.

“Together, I think that we are mapping a vibrant, dynamic, and bold economic future, one that will pioneer new horizons for the entire world,” Rubio said at a reception for participants late Friday.

The traditional group photo was canceled

Rubio had opened the talks Friday with an appeal for unity, but then canceled the traditional group photo of the foreign ministers. European diplomats objected to the inclusion of Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and threatened to boycott or walk out of the snapshot.

Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski, who was representing his country for the first time as a guest at a G20 meeting, said he was not surprised by the failure to reach consensus.

“We have wars going on, and we have participants in those wars around the table, so it’s very difficult to agree on a common line,” he told reporters.

Other participants in the meetings, who spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the private talks, said all had agreed to try to overcome their differences despite the long odds and to work to make next month’s summit of G20 leaders hosted by President Donald Trump in Florida a success.

These diplomats said a chairman’s statement had long been the plan because of the deep differences among the attendees, including over the Russia-Ukraine war, the U.S.-Israeli war against Iran, U.S. tariffs and Chinese moves in the South China Sea. All have contributed to spikes in the global cost of fuel as well as uncertainty about trade.

They described the opening session as cordial but said Russia’s position on Ukraine was unyielding — as was its opposition to Poland’s membership in the G20. They said the disagreements also included the Trump administration’s decision not to invite G20 member South Africa over the objections of others.

Russia’s presence at G20 gathering roils Europeans

Before the meeting began, some G20 countries had expressed concern that the U.S. invited Russia during its heightened attacks on Ukraine, especially after Trump pushed ties with Ukraine to new lows by waiving fresh sanctions on Moscow and temporarily limiting logistical and intelligence assistance.

The State Department, however, announced Friday that it had approved a proposed $2.1 billion sale of Patriot air defense interceptors for Ukraine.

Lavrov said Moscow would not allow a situation in which Ukraine poses a “military threat to legitimate security interests of the Russian Federation.”

Lavrov has traveled only infrequently to NATO member states since Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022 and resulting severe sanctions were imposed on Moscow. It is unclear whether Russian President Vladimir Putin will accept Trump’s invitation to attend the summit at Trump’s Miami golf club in December.

The Iran war hangs over the G20 event

Also hanging over the G20 meeting was the prospect of new U.S. military intervention in Iran. That has put the region, including G20 member Saudi Arabia, on edge. Rubio met on Friday with his Saudi counterpart, Prince Faisal bin Farhan, as well as Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, whose country has been a key mediator.

The Associated Press has reported that U.S. officials have told several Middle East countries and regional allies to be prepared for potential renewed military strikes against Iran after U.S. elections Tuesday and the possibility that Iran will retaliate.

Canada’s wildfires could cause more than $2 trillion in climate damage

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Ask what a wildfire costs and the answer usually comes back as firefighting bills and burned timber.

In a new projection of Canada’s wildfire costs through 2100, firefighting, lost timber and recreation account for only about 2% of the estimated combined costs.

Nearly all the rest comes from the climate damage associated with carbon dioxide released by the fires.

In what researchers describe as a realistic worst-case scenario, that running total passes $2.1 trillion Canadian by 2100.

Sian Kou-Giesbrecht, an assistant professor of resource and environmental management at Simon Fraser University, led the work.

Her co-authors were Renée Hall at SFU and two scientists at Environment and Climate Change Canada. Their study is a simulation, not a measurement.

A computer model of Canada’s forests, weather and fires ran from 1850 to 2100 under three possible futures.

Then it ran again with the climate frozen at pre-industrial conditions. The difference between the two runs represents the modeled contribution of climate change.

“Societal and economic costs of wildfire are highly dependent on the actions that we take to mitigate climate change right now,” Kou-Giesbrecht said.

Lower warming limits damage

One future keeps warming under 3.6°F (2°C), the Paris Agreement target.

There, climate change had no detectable effect on how much of Canada burned, how many people were exposed, or what it cost by the end of the century. All of those stayed close to recent levels.

The two hotter futures, with warming of 5.4 to 7.2°F (3 to 4°C) and 7.2 to 9°F (4 to 5°C), looked different.

By the end of the century, climate change had roughly doubled the area burned each year in the middle path and tripled it in the hottest.

That is an extra 14.6 million acres (5.9 million hectares) a year in the middle path and 19.5 million acres (7.9 million hectares) in the hottest.

For scale, the record 2023 season burned 37 million acres (15 million hectares), seven times the 1986 to 2022 average.

“In somewhat good climate news, we don’t think we’ll hit a rise of 4 or 5 C, largely because of green energy developments happening primarily outside of Canada,” Kou-Giesbrecht said.

“A global temperature rise between 3 and 4 C is a more realistic worst-case scenario.”

Carbon dwarfs the direct costs

In that middle path, climate change added about $866 million a year in direct costs by the end of the century, almost all of it lost timber.

Over 2015 to 2100, the direct total came to about $46 billion. Every figure is in 2025 Canadian dollars, and future costs were discounted at 2% a year, the rate Canada’s Treasury Board uses.

Carbon emissions account for far greater estimated damage.

Canada already prices the worldwide damage from a tonne of carbon dioxide, from crop losses to sea level rise to illness, as the social cost of carbon.

Applied to the extra wildfire emissions, climate change added $42.2 billion a year by 2080 to 2100 in the middle path and $50.2 billion in the hottest.

Over the century, those come to $2.09 trillion and $2.43 trillion.

Together, the direct costs and carbon damage amount to approximately $2.14 trillion by 2100.

That damage is spread across the whole planet, because carbon dioxide warms it wherever the gas is released.

Exposure more than triples

The team counted a person as exposed when a fire burned at least 1% of a map square about 31 miles (50 kilometers) across, roughly the distance smoke studies use.

This measure reflects proximity to wildfire activity, rather than confirmed exposure to smoke or direct danger.

By the end of the century, climate change added 72,000 exposed people a year in the middle path and 178,000 in the hottest, more than triple the number without it.

The burden also shifts between generations. Someone born in the 1960s had a 9% chance of living through a wildfire exposure.

For someone born in the 2020s, the model estimates that chance at 14% in the low-warming future, 25% in the middle path and 22% in the hottest.

The exposed places were not a cross-section of the country. About 78% of the populated areas that fires reached were primarily Indigenous communities, which make up 17% of populated areas overall.

That held in every future the team ran.

Firefighting and denser settlement did push the other way, cutting burned area by 14% to 26% depending on the path, but the climate effect was far larger.

What the model leaves out

Kou-Giesbrecht said the numbers are likely conservative, and the paper lists why.

The model captures long-term trends, not single extreme seasons like 2023, when more than 200 communities and some 232,000 people were evacuated.

Under the hottest path, an average year by 2100 approaches that season’s total.

The direct costs count only firefighting, timber and a single year of lost recreation in protected areas.

Health care, evacuations, damaged homes and roads, and business interruption are all left out, and so is smoke that travels farther than 31 miles, though Canadian smoke has reached Europe.

A lower discount rate would substantially increase the estimated cumulative costs.

Carbon that may not come back

One more gap matters for the carbon total. The model assumes forests grow back after they burn, and a growing body of field evidence shows boreal forests failing to regenerate after severe fires.

The authors argue that the social cost of carbon itself should include wildfire damage, which it currently omits. A recent United States study estimated the increase at 74%.

How much carbon burned boreal forests can eventually recover remains a major uncertainty.

If more forests fail to regenerate, the long-term climate damage could be substantially greater than these projections suggest.

The full study was published in the journal Environmental Research: Climate.

New York City Marathon 2026: How to watch live coverage, track runners, see the route map, and more

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

On Sunday, November 1, the Big Apple will be taken over by running enthusiasts.

The TCS New York City Marathon is back for the 50th anniversary of its expanded course. Here’s everything you need to know, from the history of the race to road closures, as well as how to tune in and cheer from the comfort of your couch.

A brief history of the NYC Marathon

One hundred and twenty-seven runners began the first New York City Marathon on September 13, 1970. Fifty-five finished the race located in Manhattan’s Central Park, which was put on by the New York Road Runners (NYRR). It cost just a dollar to enter and $1,000 to execute.

Beginning in 1976, the course expanded to include all five New York City boroughs. This change was made to celebrate America’s 200th birthday and was so popular that it stuck.

The race continued even in times of adversity, showing the strength and resilience of the city. For instance, runners gave their all just two months after the September 11 attacks in 2001. During the Covid-19 pandemic, the marathon went virtual.

In 2024, it became the largest marathon in history with 55,646 finishers, according to NYRR. The New York City Marathon broke its own record the following year with more than 59,000 finishers.

This year, there are nearly 60,000 hopefuls ready to take their mark.

The 411 on the map and course

The race begins at Fort Wadsworth in Staten Island. Runners head immediately to the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge.

From there, they head to Brooklyn. Miles 13 to 15 are spent in Queens, crossing the Pulaski Bridge.

Next up, the first of two legs are in Manhattan. A quick detour to the Bronx follows before returning to Manhattan and making a big finish in Central Park.

You can check out the marathon’s full map and course on NYRR’s website.

There’s also an interactive version here.

What about road closures?

Because of this expansive course, there will be many road closures, parking restrictions, and bus diversions.

For a full list of these disruptions click here and get the latest details from the city’s events coordination office.

One of the most significant closures is that of the  Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge. This begins with the upper level at 11 p.m. ET Saturday evening. The lower level will follow suit the following morning at 7 a.m. ET.

Additionally, the Bay Street and Lily Pond Avenue exits on Staten Island will be closed at 3 a.m. ET Sunday but reopen by 4 p.m. ET.

Also, do not rely on the Central Park transverse roads at 65th, 79th, and 96th streets, because they too will be closed.

How can I track a runner’s progress?

The official app from New York Road Runners lets you follow your favorite runners. You can also check leaderboards, learn about race results, and watch live coverage. Find it here.

Which celebrities are running the race?

Many celebrities from film, television, and Broadway will be pounding the pavement.

Academy Award winner Jennifer Connelly will be among the runners hoping to go all the way. Connelly is an old pro, having run the race in 2024 and finishing with a 3:45:47 timestamp. She is running to support Fred’s Team, a charity that supports the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center.

Media personality and journalist T.J. Holmes is also no stranger to the marathon. This will be his fourth race.

Queer Eye alum Antoni Porowski will give it his all to support City Harvest, while Reading Rainbow’s new host, Mychal Threets, is running to support the CMAK Sandy Hook Memorial Foundation.

Broadway star Jordan Litz ran the 2025 marathon and then did two shows as Fiyero in Wicked. This year, he is flying in from London’s West End to do the run. Thankfully, no post-race performances are necessary. Another alum of the Great White Way, Patina Miller, is also back for more.

How can I watch or stream the race live?

If you don’t want to lace up your shoes and get in on the action, you can cheer on others from the comfort of your couch.

If you are located in the New York area, WABC-TV, Channel 7 will cover the race beginning bright and early at 7 a.m. ET. You can also utilize ABC7NY.com for a live view of the finish line from 10:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. ET.

For those outside of the area, you can turn to ESPN2 from 8:00 a.m. to 11:30 a.m. ET for live coverage.

In New York, you can also listen to a race-day broadcast on iHeartRadio’s 710 WOR AM and 104.3HD2, beginning at 7:00 a.m. ET.

Or check it out on the iHeartRadio app starting at 8:00 a.m. ET.
