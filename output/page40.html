Europa Universalis 5’s Across the Pillars DLC adds nice Iberian flavors but lacks a little spice

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Europa Universalis 5‘s new DLC, Across the Pillars, focuses on adding flavor to the Iberian Peninsula, specifically Castille (Spain), Granada, and Morocco. It tremendously increases the value of playing in this wonderful part of Europe but ultimately falls short of giving you enough unique events and decisions that really turn the gameplay around.

I spent most of my time playing as Castille, looking to proclaim the Spanish Monarchy after uniting the crown of Aragon with my own, following the historical path that led to the eventual rise (and fall) of the Spanish Empire. The DLC opens the game up with a unique situation, the Battle for the Strait, in which Morocco and Castille vie for control over the last remnants of Al-Andalus—the Emirate of Granada. Castille, aka myself, wants to conquer and integrate it into the later Spain, while Morocco has its own agenda and seeks to either expand itself or reclaim the glory of the Umayyads in Iberia.

The new situation is kind of dry, but still good to have. Image via Paradox Interactive

It took several tries, but I eventually conquered Granada fully. It declared an independence war, which I had trouble containing long enough for Morocco to sweep in, proclaim Granada its subject, and take over as the war leader. The independence war no longer being the main CB, I was allowed to conquer Granada’s territories. It was rather easy since I had total continental dominance compared to Morocco, even if they held the upper hand on the sea at the time.

But to my surprise the situation just… ended? The events leading up to it were cool and flavored, but the situation itself simply had very few decisions you could make as Castille. For example, I could only dismantle Granada’s forts, reduce its sovereignty, and demand Muslim scholars from it and convert them to Christianity. That was about it, though I could also pressure its court to gain greater control over the country. Furthermore, Granada simply wants to be independent from you, and nothing really helped. Even at +150 relations, Granada was severely disloyal and declared numerous independence wars against me.

The new expeditions were an actual blast, though. Image via Paradox Interactive

Morocco and Tlemcen also were super warlike and kept declaring wars, disrupting my gameplay, for no particular reason. They couldn’t take any territories, seldom had naval supremacy, and never, and I mean never, had any upper hand on land. The Battle for the Strait thus devolves into a waiting game whereby you actually want to give Granada over to Morocco and then conquer it willy-nilly, since Granada’s countless independence wars do not allow you to do basically anything against it in a peace deal, forcing white peace.

A much stronger side of the DLC are the new expeditions, which all tie into your historical status as a colonial nation searching far and wide for mythical cities and locations. These expeditions are a much better way of exploring than simply sending out exploration missions as they come with a ton of flavor and unique events and can lead to some incredible finds and rapid expansion into the New World. As someone who absolutely adores colonial gameplay in every EU game, I found this to be the strongest and most interesting part of the DLC.

The Iberian Union did not work out well for me since for some reason Aragon kept taking it over despite my ruler being the only son of the king and queen who formed it. I eventually gained control somehow and started integrating the Catalonians into the ultimate Spanish state. A few of my runs fell through because of France, whose levy-spam strategy still works in the game, and because I am completely dumb in economics.

Overall, though, I think this DLC definitely improves the state of affairs in Iberia. It’s better than not having it, even if the main situation is a bit dry. The DLC spans hundreds of in-game years, through many different eras and ages, and it will keep you engaged and entertained for a very long time indeed.

Mitsubishi Pajero Has 5,500 Pre-Orders Before It Goes on Sale

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Mitsubishi has confirmed that the all-new Pajero (or Montero as it’s known in some markets) will go on sale in Japan on December 17.

The flagship cross-country SUV is already shaping up to be a success, as the automaker has announced it has received approximately 5,500 pre-orders for the new Pajero from its home market. The number of pre-orders has exceeded Mitsubishi’s target of 4,000 units for the fiscal year 2026 (April 2026 through March 2027).

It Starts at $39,300 in Japan—Very Close to Toyota Land Cruiser 300

Mitsubishi Motors

It does help that the Pajero has a very competitive base price of 6,193,000 yen, the equivalent of $39,300 at the current exchange rate. That’s marginally cheaper than the diesel-powered Toyota Land Cruiser 300, its direct rival.

The all-new Pajero will be available in four trims in Japan: Super Exceed, Exceed, GSR, and GLS. The former two trims are more luxury oriented, while the latter two are more focused on off-road capability.

Mitsubishi

The Japanese market will get a single powertrain—a 2.4-liter turbo diesel engine rated at 201 horsepower at 3,500 rpm and 354 pound-feet of torque from 1,750 to 2,500 rpm. Mated to an 8-speed automatic transmission with Sport mode, the engine is said to return a fuel economy of 12.1 km/L on the WLTC cycle, which is the equivalent of 28.5 MPG.

The transmission sends power to all four wheels via the Super Select 4WD-II (SS4-II) system, which allows drivers to select from four modes: 2H (rear-wheel drive), 4H (full-time 4WD), 4HLc (locked center differential), and 4LLc (locked center differential with low-range gears). According to Mitsubishi, the SS4-II system combines “high off-road capability with excellent on-road handling stability.”

Truck-Based Ladder-Frame Chassis Provides Solid Base for Off-Road Capability

Mitsubishi

The AWD system is integrated into the Super-All Wheel Control (S-AWC) vehicle dynamics control system, which controls driving, cornering and braking.

S-AWC also integrates control of Active Yaw Control (AYC), which uses the front brakes to control torque between the left and right wheels, Active Stability and Traction Control (ASTC), which distributes driving force to enhance stability on slippery surfaces, and ABS.

Mitsubishi

The new Pajero offers seven selectable drive modes for specific driving conditions: Normal, Eco, Gravel, Snow, Mud, Sand and Rock. The Pajero is based on the ladder-frame chassis from the Mitsubishi Triton pickup, which is a great prerequisite for off-road capability.

Models equipped with 20-inch tires (standard on Super Exceed and Exceed trims) offer 9 inches (230 mm) of ground clearance, a 30.4-degree approach angle, a 22.6-degree ramp breakover angle, and a 25.8-degree departure angle.

The new Pajero is already on sale in Thailand and will launch in Australia later this year before expanding to around 100 other international markets. The United States has not been confirmed among those markets, but Mitsubishi previewed a body-on-frame flagship SUV to U.S. dealers under the Montero name. If the Montero does arrive stateside in the coming years it will definitely need a different powertrain to meet U.S. emissions certification.

Influencer Allison Kuch Will Run NYC Marathon 6 Months Postpartum

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Influencer Allison Kuch is proving, once again, that moms really can do it all — including running the 2026 TCS New York City Marathon just six months after giving birth.

“Six months postpartum, still haven’t slept through the night, mastitis, missed training runs and I’m still here!!!” Kuch, 31, wrote via Instagram on Friday, October 30, sharing footage of herself picking up her race bib. “So excited to run with maybelline in my first marathon and running FOR mental health!!”

Kuch, who gave birth to daughter Pepper in April, will tackle the 26.2-mile course throughout the five boroughs of New York City on Sunday, November 1.

“I can’t give up on my dreams, my daughters are watching,” she added via Instagram.

How Allison Kuch's Husband Is Helping During Post-Baby 'Identity Crisis'

Kuch and her husband, Isaac Rochell, are also parents of 2-year-old daughter Scottie. Rochell, 31, and the couple’s two girls will be cheering on Kuch on race day.

“Made it to the expo!!!” Kuch wrote via her Instagram Stories on Friday, going to collect up her registration gear. “Crazy that 60,000 people come through here and pick up their bibs lol.”

Kuch also made a point to visit the race’s official Lactation Support booth.

View this post on Instagram

“Fun fact — there are ‘lactation stations’ along the route, so you don’t have to pack a [breast] pump,” she explained. “They have pumps provided at the start line and there’s three stops on the route.”

Kuch, who is still nursing baby Pepper, has been candid about her postpartum experience.

“When people ask, ‘How are you doing postpartum?’ My brain goes to two places: sleep and mental health,” Kuch told Rochell on the latest episode of their “Couldn’t Agree Less” Netflix podcast. “Here’s my issue with postpartum: Everyone worries about the baby, about birth [in the moment]. … Four months postpartum, nobody’s checking in, and I would say that’s the hardest time of postpartum.”

Pregnant Allison Kuch ‘Can’t Wait’ to Get a Boob Job After Having Kids

She continued, “Four months postpartum is hard because you’re out of newborn-ness, your baby’s more aware, they don’t want to be set down, you’re going through the four-month sleep regression, you’re still breast-feeding. People are expecting you to be back and, like, mama is not back.”

According to Kuch, her postpartum experience has been widely different after each birth journey.

“I think probably trying to do it all [is my biggest challenge now],” she candidly admitted. “It’s a me issue, I think, actually. I just feel like I want to be back as much as other people are assuming I’m back. I wanna wake up and go work out and have a morning routine and put a skincare product on my face. Then, when I wake up, I’m like, ‘But, she’s only so little for this long.’ I feel like I should give her all of my time.”
